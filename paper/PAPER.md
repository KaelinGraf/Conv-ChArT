# ConChArT: A Convolutional–Transformer Hybrid Network for Robust Fiducial Pose Estimation

---

## Abstract

Learned, marker-specific fiducial detectors have demonstrated decisive robustness over classical ChArUco detection in low-signal-to-noise regimes: Deep ChArUco [Hu2019] reports 100% vs. 0% pose detection at 0.3–1 lux, 100% vs. 36% in shadow, 78% vs. 27% under motion blur, and 97.4% vs. 68.8% pose accuracy at a 3 px reprojection threshold across its full 26-video test set (Fig. 12) — credited here on the paper's own terms, not received wisdom. That robustness, however, sits inside a narrow *scale* regime: a fixed low-resolution operating point ($320\times240$, [Hu2019, §3.1]), a narrow working-distance envelope, no partial-view close-range operation, and a convolutional-only receptive field that cannot carry a corner's *identity* once the board's apparent scale exceeds it (§3.3, Appendix A). Separately, the field's training formulations grant no partial credit near the target: SuperPoint's 65-way cell-softmax head, inherited by ChArUcoNet, and RefineNet's 4096-way sub-pixel softmax both cost a 1-pixel miss and a 30-pixel miss the same penalty. This paper presents **ConChArT** (Convolutional ChArUco Transformer), targeting both gaps with four contributions: (1) bottleneck self-attention with axial rotary position embeddings, closing the receptive-field gap; (2) sixteen independent per-channel penalty-reduced Gaussian-focal objectives, replacing cell-softmax cross-entropy with graded partial reward inside each target disc; (3) a RANSAC lattice-consistency gate converting a wrong learned identity into a detected outlier rather than a silent pose error; and (4) tilt-calibrated homography synthesis with envelope-spanning domain randomisation. A compact edge-deployment application — module docking under active-illumination differencing — is evaluated for compute feasibility. Every claim is design-motivated and measured at the component level; end-to-end accuracy results are pending (§5.3, §5.4).

## 1. Introduction

Fiducial-marker pose estimation — recovering a camera's pose relative to a printed board of known geometry — is a solved problem under nominal conditions: classical ChArUco detection (`cv2.aruco`) is fast, accurate, and requires no training data. It fails, however, exactly where robotics applications most need it to succeed: low light, motion blur, and partial occlusion. Deep ChArUco [Hu2019] established that a learned detector closes this gap decisively. Read directly from the paper rather than from the version of it that gets cited: Hu et al. tested illuminance from 0.3 to 700 lux, plus shadow and motion blur, and report 100% vs. 0% pose detection at 0.3–1 lux, 100% vs. 36% detection in shadow, 78% vs. 27% under motion blur, and — aggregated over their full 26-video test set at a 3 px reprojection-error threshold — 97.4% vs. 68.8% pose-estimation accuracy (Fig. 12) — a wide, and honestly earned, margin. The paper also states explicitly that classical detection is *not* broken in ordinary light: *"For a broad range of 'bright enough' scenarios ranging from 3 lux to 700 lux, both Deep ChArUco and OpenCV successfully detect a pose 100% of the time."* The case for a learned detector is the dark, blurred, and shadowed tail of the illuminance distribution, not a blanket claim that classical detection is obsolete, and this paper credits that evidence on its own terms rather than the inflated version of it that circulates by citation.

That said, Deep ChArUco's demonstrated robustness sits inside a narrow *scale* regime, and this is the gap this paper addresses. Three limitations compound:

- **A fixed, low-resolution operating point.** ChArUcoNet processes fixed $320\times240$ input images, reported to run at approximately 100 fps at that resolution [Hu2019, §3.1], and inherits SuperPoint's [DeTone2018] $8\times8$-cell decomposition for its localisation head. Nothing in the published evaluation exercises a working-distance range wide enough to test whether the same network generalises to a board occupying a handful of pixels at long range or the majority of the frame at close range.
- **No partial-view, close-range operation.** A board that only partially fills the frame — the common case at close range, where a docking or manipulation task needs the *most* precision — is outside the demonstrated envelope entirely.
- **A convolutional-only receptive field that cannot carry corner identity at large apparent scale.** A ChArUco board's sixteen inner corners are locally indistinguishable — a bare checkerboard junction is $90°$-rotation invariant, and identity is encoded entirely in the surrounding ArUco markers, not in local corner appearance. Recovering identity from *context* — "this corner sits two lattice cells from a legible marker" — requires a receptive field that reaches board scale. A convolutional stack's receptive field is a fixed function of its depth; as the board's apparent size grows, the fraction of it that a fixed receptive field can see shrinks, and past a measurable point (§3.3; the full arithmetic is in Appendix A) local evidence alone cannot resolve identity at all. This is not a training-data problem — no amount of additional supervision fixes a structural ceiling on what information reaches a given output unit.

A second, independent gap sits in *how* every detector in this lineage is trained. SuperPoint's localisation head is a 65-way softmax over sub-cell positions (64 positions plus an explicit dustbin) per $8\times8$ px cell; ChArUcoNet inherits it verbatim, stating so directly (*"We use the same point localization head as SuperPoint"*). Deep ChArUco's own sub-pixel stage, RefineNet, is a 4096-way softmax classification over an $8\times$-enlarged region. Both are one-hot cross-entropy objectives: a prediction one grid cell from the true target and a prediction thirty cells away are both simply *wrong*, penalised identically, with no notion that "close" should cost less than "far." This discards ordinal structure the target space actually has — a corner *is* physically nearer to some wrong answers than others — and it is a training-formulation choice, not a labelling constraint; nothing about the underlying geometry requires a one-hot target.

This paper presents **ConChArT** (Convolutional ChArUco Transformer), a hybrid convolutional–transformer detector, sub-pixel refiner, and classical pose stage built to close both gaps, with contributions ordered by the argument above:

1. **Scale-robust corner identity via bottleneck attention.** A convolutional encoder supplies high-resolution, translation-equivariant features for *localisation*; a compact self-attention bottleneck with two-dimensional axial rotary position embeddings (RoPE) supplies the board-scale context *identity* needs, closing the receptive-field gap the arithmetic above quantifies. This is a receptive-field-closure argument, not merely an empirical add-on — §3.3 derives it and Appendix A tabulates it; ablation A1 (§5.4) is designed to measure it directly once run.
2. **Per-channel penalty-reduced Gaussian supervision, replacing cell-softmax cross-entropy.** Sixteen independent per-corner-index sigmoid channels, each trained with a penalty-reduced Gaussian-focal objective, replace the field's inherited one-hot softmax convention. Every wrong channel is penalised everywhere and the correct channel is forgiven inside a Gaussian disc around the target — graded partial reward within the disc, full penalty outside it — which preserves ordinal structure in the loss landscape that a softmax cannot express, and licenses the *permissive-not-prescriptive* class supervision policy of §3.4 (a corner may honestly report "present, identity unknown" rather than being forced to guess).
3. **A geometric lattice-consistency gate.** A RANSAC-fit homography between the board's known canonical lattice and the detected image points is the sole independent check on every learned identity, converting a confidently wrong identity into a detected, demoted outlier rather than a silently wrong pose (§3.6).
4. **Tilt-calibrated homography synthesis and envelope-spanning domain randomisation.** Training data is $100\%$ synthetic, generated by a compositing engine whose warp sampler composes a first-order-derived, empirically verified perspective factor with a standard affine sampler — genuine out-of-plane foreshortening, not affine shear alone — and whose scale sampling deliberately spans the full apparent-size envelope this paper's contributions (1) and (2) are built to cover, from small, context-dependent corners to partial, close-range, frame-clipping views (§4).

Beyond the four contributions above, a compact deployment application — module docking under active-illumination lit/dark differencing, the case study that originally motivated this system — is evaluated for edge-compute feasibility against an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin budget (§6). It is presented as an application of the general-purpose detector above, not as this paper's framing: the contributions are scale-robust fiducial detection and partial-credit supervision, evaluated first on their own terms.

Every quantitative claim in this paper is either a measured component-level number (parameter counts, latency, compute, generation-quality audit statistics, coordinate-transform correctness) or a design argument awaiting the end-to-end accuracy run that would confirm it; §5.3 and §5.4 state plainly which is which, and no claim about real-world detection accuracy is made anywhere in this paper.

## 2. Related Work

This section states, for every prior method this system's design draws on, exactly what is taken, what is replaced or changed, and the documented reason for each departure. Every grounding claim below was independently re-verified against the primary source (paper full text and, where one exists, the reference implementation) as a dedicated verification pass, described in full in Appendix E; divergences are marked **[kept]**, **[replaced — reason given]**, or **[deviates — reason given]**.

A generic corner or keypoint detector has no notion of *which* of sixteen indistinguishable lattice junctions it has found; a bare checkerboard is $90°$-rotation invariant, and corner identity on a ChArUco board is carried entirely by the surrounding ArUco markers, not by local corner appearance. Recovering identity therefore requires either (a) reading the marker code directly, which needs enough resolved pixels-per-module to be legible and fails exactly in the dark/blur/large-apparent-scale regime this paper targets, or (b) inferring identity from board-scale context — "this corner sits two lattice cells from a legible marker" — which is a relative-geometry inference a network can make even when the nearest marker is itself illegible or out of a fixed receptive field's reach, provided the network's own receptive field reaches board scale. This system's factorised heatmap/class-head design and its attention bottleneck exist to make (b) trainable at all, and the discussion below states, prior method by prior method, why a generic keypoint pipeline was not simply reused.

**Deep ChArUco** [Hu2019] is the direct precedent and the only prior system solving this exact problem (a marker-specific ChArUco corner-and-pose pipeline trained substantially on synthetic/augmented data). Its ChArUcoNet detector borrows SuperPoint's [DeTone2018] cell-softmax localisation head verbatim — *"We use the same point localization head as SuperPoint"* — outputting a 65-way (64 sub-cell positions plus an explicit dustbin) distribution per $8\times 8$ px cell, paired with a 17-way (16 corner IDs plus dustbin) ID head at the same $H/8$ grid. **[replaced — reason given]** This system replaces both heads with a full-resolution sigmoid heatmap for localisation (no cell quantisation at all; refinement is a separate stage) and sixteen independent per-index sigmoid channels for identity, with no explicit dustbin class — the all-channels-low state *is* the dustbin, which is what makes "corner detected, identity unknown" directly expressible rather than a forced argmax among seventeen labels (§3.4). Deep ChArUco's RefineNet — the $24^2\to64^2$ @ $8\times$ sub-pixel refiner architecture, the $\pm4$ px capture range (a corner guaranteed within the central $8\times8$ px region of a $24\times24$ crop), and the one-corner-per-crop synthetic training scheme — is adopted structurally (§3.5). **[replaced — reason given]** Its *mechanism* is not: the paper's RefineNet performs "softmax classification over an 8× enlarged central region … a 4096-way classification problem" through an FC bottleneck; this system instead uses a fully convolutional network producing a heatmap trained with the same penalty-reduced focal loss as the detector, read out by a windowed soft-argmax. The reason is architectural consistency (one loss form across every head) and export cleanliness (a fully convolutional, PixelShuffle-based upsampling path has no FC bottleneck to fold and is TensorRT-clean) — and this deviation has code precedent even within the Deep ChArUco lineage: the unofficial reference implementation [JunkyByte] itself abandoned the paper's FC-bottleneck design for a fully convolutional one, using stacked nearest-neighbour upsamples in place of the paper's softmax classifier. One attribution correction this verification pass surfaced and records here for the reader: the commonly repeated "$\approx 12$ M training composites" exposure figure for this lineage does not appear in the Deep ChArUco paper at all (its positive corpus is $7{,}955$ real video frames, augmented on the fly; only RefineNet's corpus is synthetic) — the $\approx 12$ M figure reconstructs exactly from the unofficial reference implementation's own checkpoint naming ($369{,}700$ steps $\times$ batch 32 $\approx 11.83$ M), and this system's own training-exposure budget (§5.1) is correctly attributed to that implementation, not the paper.

**CornerNet** [Law2018] **and CenterNet** [Zhou2019] supply the loss and decode machinery this system's heatmap and class heads actually run on. **[kept]** The penalty-reduced focal form, $\alpha=2$, $\beta=4$, and the convention of combining multiple corners' Gaussian splats onto a shared canvas by per-pixel maximum rather than summation are lifted verbatim and verified against both the CornerNet paper text and the reference decode code; the $3\times 3$ max-pool-equality peak-extraction rule is likewise byte-for-byte identical to CenterNet's published `_nms` implementation. **[deviates — reason given]** Two numeric departures are recorded: this system uses a fixed $\sigma_{\text{hm}}=2$ px Gaussian width rather than CornerNet's object-size-derived radius (a box-size construct that does not apply to dimensionless corners, so a fixed width is the natural degeneration — every unattributed $\sigma$/threshold in this paper is this project's own design choice, not a citation); and the loss is computed in logit space via a numerically stable `logsigmoid` form rather than the reference's clamped-probability form (`clamp(sigmoid(x), 1e-4, 1-1e-4)`), because a naive $\log(1-\hat p)$ term NaNs under bf16 mixed-precision training near a forced-exact target of 1.0 — a training-numerics constraint the CornerNet/CenterNet codebases, trained in fp32, never had to solve. **[deviates — reason given]** The final logit-layer bias is initialised to $-2.19$ (equivalent to an initial positive prior $\pi = 0.1$), a deliberate departure from the $-4.59$ ($\pi=0.01$) value this loss lineage inherits from RetinaNet's focal-loss framing [Lin2017]: RetinaNet's prior was tuned for eighty sparse object classes over a dense anchor grid, whereas this system's sixteen class channels and single heatmap channel are dense, per-pixel targets covering the entire map — a much higher expected positive density justifies a less extreme initial prior.

**TransUNet** [Chen2021] is the source of the hybrid macro-topology — convolutional encoder feeding a transformer over the encoder's own feature-map tokens, with a cascaded, skip-connected upsampling decoder. **[kept]** The macro-pattern (CNN for high-resolution spatial detail, transformer for long-range dependency over CNN tokens, U-Net-style decoder) is taken as topology reference only — its pretrained-ViT loading and segmentation harness are not part of this system's implementation. A final LayerNorm after the transformer blocks, before the tokens re-enter the decoder, is retained because both TransUNet's official implementation and timm's Vision Transformer apply exactly this normalisation before their respective heads, even though neither paper's text states it as an equation. **[replaced — reason given]** TransUNet tokenises via a learned $1\times 1$ convolutional projection and adds learned absolute position embeddings; this system uses pure reshape (no projection — the trunk's channel width is chosen to equal the embedding dimension $d$ so none is needed) and no absolute position embeddings at all, relying entirely on the relative-offset RoPE mechanism (§3.3). The U-Net [Ronneberger2015] block shapes (paired $3\times3$-conv/BN/ReLU, max-pool between stages) follow the widely used community reference implementation's [milesial] bilinear-upsample variant rather than the original paper's valid-padded, batchnorm-free convolutions and transposed-convolution upsampling — the decoder never uses a transposed convolution anywhere, on the documented rationale below.

**Dilated convolutions** [Yu2016] justify the two dilated $3\times3$ convolutions (rates 2, 4) appended to the final encoder stage; the hybrid-dilation-condition analysis of Wang et al. [Wang2018] confirms this specific rate schedule, preceded by two rate-1 convolutions, produces no gridding artifact even though the (2, 4) pair alone would share a common factor — a fact this project's own verification pass computed and records here, since neither cited source states it for this exact configuration (Appendix A).

**The banned transposed convolution.** The Distill article on checkerboard artifacts [Odena2016] documents the uneven-kernel-overlap mechanism that makes transposed convolutions produce stride-periodic artifacts — directly fatal against a checkerboard-patterned target — and is the basis for this system's decoder using only bilinear upsampling followed by an ordinary convolution at every stage. **[deviates — reason given]** The same article's own empirical recommendation is *nearest-neighbour*, not bilinear, resampling (*"We've had our best results with nearest-neighbor interpolation, and had difficulty making bilinear resize work"*); this system uses bilinear anyway, on the strength of the two reference codebases it otherwise follows (both the community U-Net implementation and TransUNet's official decoder use bilinear upsampling), and because the Distill article's difficulty case is a generative decoder synthesising content from nothing, whereas every upsample here is immediately followed by a concatenation with a full-resolution skip connection that re-injects high-frequency detail rather than asking the upsample to invent it.

**RoFormer** [Su2021] **and RoPE-ViT** [Heo2024] supply the rotary position-encoding mechanism: rotating query and key vectors by a position-dependent phase so their dot product depends only on content and *relative* offset, verified directly against both papers' equations and against the official RoPE-ViT reference implementation's axial frequency construction. **[kept]** The half-dimension-per-axis axial split, row-major token ordering (matching the encoder's own `flatten` order), and the absence of any absolute position embedding are all confirmed to match the reference implementation's own defaults, not merely the paper text. **[deviates — reason given, non-negotiable]** The standard base-parameterised frequency schedule ($\theta_i = \text{base}^{-i/(d/4)}$) cannot express this system's own requirement that RoPE wavelengths span from $2$ token cells up to at least $2\times$ the token-grid extent: under that schedule the fastest frequency is always $\theta_{\max}=\text{base}^0=1$ rad/cell regardless of base, so the shortest achievable wavelength is $2\pi\approx 6.28$ cells, never 2 — a fact confirmed directly from the reference implementation's own frequency-generating code, not merely asserted. This system instead anchors the spectrum directly in wavelengths (§3.3, Appendix B); the anchor is nudged to $2.5$ rather than exactly $2$ because $\lambda=2$ is Nyquist-degenerate (a phase increment of exactly $\pi$ per cell collapses that frequency pair to a bare sign-alternating parity bit, discarding its positional information). This deliberately sacrifices the paper's cross-resolution frequency-extrapolation property, which is an explicit, accepted trade: the trained checkpoint is valid only at its training input size and attend-stage, by design.

**Attention U-Net** [Oktay2018] supplies the additive gating mechanism applied to the two coarsest decoder skip connections. **[kept]** The gate equations (channel-reducing $1\times1$ convolutions on the skip and gating signal, ReLU, a further $1\times1$ to a single-channel logit, sigmoid, bilinear upsample to the skip's resolution, elementwise multiply) match the paper's Eq. 1–2 exactly, confirmed against both the paper and the official reference repository. **[deviates — reason given]** Pass-through gate initialisation (so gradients are never vetoed at the start of training) is *not* actually specified by the Attention U-Net paper's own equations, despite being commonly attributed to it; this project's verification pass traced the correct precedent to the official repository's TORR variant, which sets the final gate convolution's bias to $+3.0$ for exactly this purpose — confirmed verbatim in the fetched source. This system's implementation strengthens that precedent by *also* zeroing the same convolution's weight, so the gate output is exactly constant ($\sigma(3)\approx 0.953$, not merely biased toward it) at initialisation, with gradients still flowing into that weight from the very first step (§3.3). **[deviates — reason given]** Only the two coarsest decoder skips are gated, against the paper's convention of gating all but the very lowest-resolution skip; this system's own criterion — sharper than anything stated in the cited paper — is that gating should apply only where a wrongly suppressed skip is *recoverable* downstream: a corner whose local identity evidence is gated away can still be recovered by the lattice-projection pass (§3.6), whereas a full-resolution localisation feature vetoed by a "looks like board" gate produces an undetectable corner with no recovery path. This asymmetric-risk argument, not gate placement convention, is what this system's design rationale and its `gate_all_skips` ablation axis (§5.4, A3) exist to test.

**Integral human pose regression** [Sun2018] supplies the idea of reading a heatmap out as a probability-weighted expectation rather than a hard argmax, used for the sub-pixel refiner's soft-argmax readout. **[deviates — reason given]** Sun et al.'s formulation is a softmax expectation over the *entire* output map, trained end-to-end through the integral; this system instead computes a sigmoid-probability-weighted centroid over a fixed $5\times 5$ window around the hard argmax, applied post-hoc at inference/evaluation rather than trained through — nothing in the cited paper argues for or against a local window, so the windowed form is this project's own design, guarded operationally by the refiner content check (§4.7) rather than by any cited precedent — it borrows the expectation *idea*, not the exact formula, from Sun et al.

**Understanding the Effective Receptive Field** [Luo2016] supplies both the theoretical justification for the attention bottleneck (a convolutional stack's effective receptive field grows only as $O(\sqrt{n})$ in depth and shrinks further under skip connections and ReLU nonlinearities — confirmed directly against the paper's variance-addition derivation, Eq. 10–11) and the measurement procedure for the effective-receptive-field probe used to verify it empirically (§5.2, metric M-07): a unit gradient signal placed at one output location and back-propagated to the input, its magnitude map characterising the *effective*, as opposed to theoretical, receptive field. **[deviates — reason given]** The paper's own procedure explicitly averages the resulting gradient map over multiple random inputs (*"most of our results will be presented in terms of expectations over input distribution … averaged over 20 runs"*) and probes the centre of the output plane; this system's evaluation protocol follows both of these details precisely — averaging over at least twenty validation images and probing a central class-head unit — because a single-image probe of a trained, nonlinear network is largely input-specific noise, not a stable measure of the network's characteristic receptive field. What Luo et al.'s paper establishes cleanly, and what motivates the architectural choice rather than a conv-side patch, is that no amount of kernel re-weighting closes the reach gap: *"no matter what we do to change $w(m)$, the effective receptive field is still distributed like a Gaussian so the above proposal only solves the problem partially"* — direct textual support for routing around the conv path's ceiling with global self-attention rather than deeper dilation.

**EPro-PnP** [Chen2022] is the leading differentiable/probabilistic perspective-$n$-point formulation, allowing end-to-end gradient flow through the pose solve itself. It is deliberately not adopted in this system's current design: pose-only supervision is sparse (one scalar loss per image against a six-degree-of-freedom quantity), ill-conditioned near the planar homography's intrinsic two-fold ambiguity, and non-differentiable at the discrete plane-vs-plane solution choice that ambiguity forces — so training instead stays modular on exact intermediate labels (corner position, visibility, and identity, all of which synthesis provides for free), with pose computed only at inference by classical geometry (§3.6). Re-introducing EPro-PnP-style fine-tuning once the modular system is stable is recorded as a natural, not-yet-executed extension (§7).

**Segment Anything Model 2** [Ravi2024] supplies the automatic mask generator used entirely offline, as a data-engine tool rather than an architectural component: an object-cutout occlusion bank is built once from COCO imagery by SAM2's automatic mask proposal, filtered, and cached to disk for the synthetic compositor to draw from at training time (§4.4). No SAM2 inference happens at training or deployment time; it never enters the detector's own computational graph.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Problem formulation & system overview

ConChArT takes a single grayscale frame and returns a metric 6-DoF pose of a locked $5\times5$ ChArUco board (16 inner corners; §3.2), computed in three stages run in strict sequence, with metric scale entering the pipeline only at the final step:

```
  sensor frame ──resize (antialiased)──► Stage 1: DETECTOR
                                          hybrid conv–transformer trunk
                                          ├─► heatmap  (full res, 1ch)  "where"
                                          └─► class map (H/4,   16ch)  "which"
                       ┌──────────────────────────┘
                       │ peaks (3x3 max-pool eq., τ_hm)
                       ▼
  sensor frame ──crop, native res──────► Stage 2: REFINER (board-agnostic)
     (dashed path: bypasses the           24x24 sensor crop → 64x64 @ 8x
      resized network input entirely)     heatmap → soft-argmax → sub-px xy
                       │
                       ▼
              per-corner (x, y, p_hm) + class-map ID readout (p_id)
                       │
                       ▼
  Stage 3: undistort → LATTICE-CONSISTENCY GATE (RANSAC H, canonical↔image)
           → recovery (project canon. lattice through H, claim ID-less pts)
           → cv2.solvePnPGeneric(SOLVEPNP_IPPE)  ◄── K, dist, board file (metric scale enters here only)
                       │
                       ▼
      {rvec, tvec, reprojection RMS, corners:[{x,y,index,source,p_hm,p_id}]}
```

The dashed refiner path is deliberate: Stage 2 always cuts its $24\times24$ window from the native sensor frame, centred on the Stage-1 peak mapped back through the half-pixel resolution convention (§3.2), never from the (possibly downsampled) network input. This decouples sub-pixel accuracy from whatever input resolution Stage 1 happens to run at — a resolution chosen for compute-budget and receptive-field reasons (Appendix C), not for localisation precision.

**Factorised heads.** The central representational choice is that Stage 1 answers two questions from one trunk through two separated heads: a full-resolution heatmap for *localisation* (skip-rich, high-resolution features; every corner, visible or degraded, gets a peak) and a $16$-channel class map at $H/4$ for *identity* (globally mixed, board-scale context; §3.3). Keeping them separate — rather than a single $17$-way per-cell classification, as the lineage this system descends from uses — means the state "a corner is here, but its identity cannot currently be determined" is directly representable: heatmap confidence high, every one of the sixteen class channels low. This is not a corner case; it is the *expected* state for a large fraction of corners in the dark, blurred, partially occluded, or large-apparent-scale regime this system targets, and the ground-truth policy (§4.4) is designed specifically so the network is rewarded for reaching it honestly rather than forced to guess.

Every detected corner carries a confidence pair $(p_{\text{hm}}, p_{\text{id}})$ — a per-position heatmap score and a per-channel class-map confidence, neither of which is a calibrated posterior — and a provenance tag distinguishing a corner whose identity came directly from the class head (`source: head`) from one whose identity was assigned by the Stage-3 recovery pass (`source: recovered`), because a rising recovered fraction is the system's own early warning that the class head has left its training distribution (§3.6). The full per-frame output contract is
$$
\{\, \text{rvec},\ \text{tvec},\ \text{reprojection RMS},\ \text{corners}: [\{x, y, \text{index}, \text{source}, p_{\text{hm}}, p_{\text{id}}\}] \,\}
$$
with `rvec`/`tvec` absent and a `reason` string present whenever Stage 3 declines to produce a pose — a defined outcome (too few correspondences, a collinear or otherwise degenerate lattice fit, no intrinsics supplied, or an uncorroborated minimal fit; enumerated in full in §3.6) rather than a crash or a silently fabricated answer. This matters concretely: OpenCV's own IPPE solver wraps its internal failure path in a bare `catch(...)` and silently returns zero solutions on internal failure — confirmed directly from the OpenCV source during this project's verification pass (Appendix E) — so an empty solver result is a *reachable* run-time event that the pipeline must treat as a defined no-pose outcome, not an exception.

**Operating envelope, in brief.** Training and evaluation are parameterised by the apparent board-square side $s$ in input pixels, sampled log-uniformly over $s\in[16,128]$ px — an $8\times$ range chosen so the training and validation distribution spans from small, context-dependent corners (identity must come from the attention path or Stage-3 recovery) to large corners whose own square can exceed a conv-only receptive field, including partial views that clip the frame. The production network runs at the sensor's own native resolution ($1600\times1200$, downsample ratio $\rho=1$), a decision driven by an optics-closure equation relating focal length, sensor pitch, board size, and working distance to $s$; the full four-constraint derivation, the optics equation, and the native-resolution decision's own reasoning are in Appendix C. This envelope choice is also what forces the attention bottleneck's placement at $H/16$ rather than $H/8$ (§3.3): at native resolution, attending at $H/8$ would cost more compute than the entire rest of the network combined (Appendix A).

### 3.2 Board geometry & coordinate conventions

**Board definition.** The board is locked: a $5\times5$-square ChArUco layout, sixteen inner corners ($4\times4$), twelve markers from `DICT_5X5_50` (dictionary elements 0–11, raster order over the white squares), marker-to-square ratio $0.7$. Corner index is $i = 4\cdot\text{row}+\text{col}$, row/col $\in\{0..3\}$, row-major from the top-left, canonical orientation; the canonical (unitless) lattice coordinate for index $i$ is $(\text{col}{+}1,\ \text{row}{+}1)$, metric scale applied only by the runtime board file at PnP time. Two structural facts the pipeline relies on: every inner corner touches exactly two markers (the four surrounding squares are always two black, two white), and the *bare* checkerboard pattern is $90°$-rotation invariant — identity is carried entirely by the markers, never by local corner appearance, which is exactly why a context-only inference (§2, §3.3) is sometimes the only route to identity at all. The exhaustive board-parameter and dictionary tables are collected in Appendix C.

**Analytic corner formula.** Corner coordinates are never re-detected from a rendered image; they are computed analytically from the same geometry the renderer uses, and carried through every subsequent warp as full-precision float keypoints (integer rounding anywhere in this chain is treated as a defect). At render resolution $R$ (a multiple of $5$, square side $SQ = R/5$ px), corner $i$'s pixel position is
$$
\text{corner\_px}(i) = \Big(\big(\lfloor i \bmod 4\rfloor + 1\big)\cdot SQ - 0.5,\ \ \big(\lfloor i / 4\rfloor + 1\big)\cdot SQ - 0.5\Big),
$$
the $-0.5$ offset being the pixel-centre convention: an integer pixel coordinate is that pixel's *centre*, so a square boundary — which falls exactly between two pixels at a multiple of $SQ$ — sits $0.5$ px past the centre of pixel $SQ{-}1$ and $0.5$ px before the centre of pixel $SQ$; the formula's $-0.5$ places the corner exactly on that boundary in the pixel-centre frame. This formula was verified against OpenCV's own detection machinery, independently of the analytic derivation: `cv2.aruco.CharucoBoard.getChessboardCorners()` returns exactly the same row-major index-to-lattice mapping, locking that no ordering ambiguity survives a library-version change; classical sub-pixel refinement (`cv2.cornerSubPix`, seeded near the analytic position) converges to the analytic corner to **0.0 px** at both $480\times480$ and $960\times960$ render resolutions; and a companion check against the *marker* corners (comparing detected ArUco marker corners to their analytically expected positions) agrees to $1.10$ px, locking the marker-to-lattice orientation as well.

**The half-pixel resolution map.** Every place the system moves a coordinate between two resolutions — sensor $\leftrightarrow$ input, input $\leftrightarrow$ $H/4$ class-map cells, crop-centre $\leftrightarrow$ sensor frame — uses one convention, never a bare multiply or divide:
$$
x' = (x + 0.5)\cdot\text{scale} - 0.5,
$$
where $\text{scale}$ is the ratio of new pixels (or cells) per old one. Bare scaling ($x'_{\text{naive}} = x\cdot\text{scale}$) omits both half-pixel terms and introduces a *constant*, resolution-independent bias of $0.5(1-\text{scale})$ — at the class-map readout ($\text{scale}=1/4$) this is $0.375$ cells $=1.5$ input pixels, a systematic identity-readout error this system closes by defining the identity readout (§3.6) as an `align_corners=False` bilinear grid-sample rather than a literal division. The full derivation — why the pixel-centre convention forces this exact form, and the bias computation for an arbitrary bare-scaling error — is in Appendix B.

**Two grids.** Two distinct spatial grids appear throughout this system and are deliberately not conflated: *class cells*, the $H/4$ grid the class head reads its identity confidence from ($4$ input px per cell — an identity, not a context, resolution), and *attention cells*, the $H/16$ grid the self-attention bottleneck tokenises over ($16$ input px per cell at native resolution — the sole board-scale context mechanism in the whole system, introduced next in §3.3). A class-map cell answers "what index is the corner nearest this $4\times4$ px patch"; an attention-cell token answers "what board-scale content sits in this $16\times16$ px patch, available to be attended to from anywhere else on the board." Confusing the two — for instance, reasoning about RoPE's wavelength span in class-cell units, or about class-map bilinear readout bias in attention-cell units — produces silently wrong arithmetic; this paper uses primed variables ($H',W'$) for the attention grid and the bare `4×` grid for the class grid throughout, and every quantity that carries a cell unit states which grid it is in.

### 3.3 The ConChArT architecture

The Stage-1 network (`dcc/model.py`, class `DetectorNet`) is a hybrid conv–transformer trunk: a five-stage convolutional encoder feeding a bottleneck self-attention module with 2D axial rotary position embeddings, followed by a gated-skip decoder with the two factorised output heads of §3.1. All measurements in this section are taken at the pinned native input resolution $W\times H=1600\times1200$ (§3.1, Appendix C).

```
e1 -> e2 -> e3 -> e4 -> e5      conv encoder, H -> H/16, widths 32-64-128-256-256
                      |
                   blocks        MHSA+MLP x n_blocks, axial RoPE, over H/16 tokens
                      |
d4 <- gate4(e4) <----+          H/16 -> H/8
d3 <- gate3(e3) <- d4           H/8  -> H/4   --tap--> cls (H/4, 16ch)
d2 <-       e2  <- d3           H/4  -> H/2, ungated
d1 <-       e1  <- d2           H/2  -> H,   ungated  --tap--> hm  (H, 1ch)
```

**Encoder.** Five stages of paired $3\times3$ convolutions, each `Conv2d(pad=1, bias=False) → BatchNorm2d → ReLU`, with $2\times2$ max-pooling between stages — the standard `DoubleConv`/`Down` block shape of [Ronneberger2015]'s U-Net, taken via its `milesial/Pytorch-UNet` reference implementation:
$$
\texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(c_{in},c_{out},\delta) = \mathrm{ReLU}\big(\mathrm{BN}(\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times3,\,\mathrm{dil}=\delta,\,\mathrm{pad}=\delta}(x;\,\text{no bias}))\big), \qquad
\texttt{double\_conv}(c_{in},c_{out}) = \texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(c_{out},c_{out})\circ\texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(c_{in},c_{out})
$$
Widths run 32-64-128-256-256; e5's four convolutions are `double_conv(256,256)` followed by an explicit dilated pair (rates 2, 4) — the dilated pair sits at e5 (H/16), a placement forced by the native-resolution migration discussed under compute budget below. The encoder's own theoretical receptive field, by the standard recursive formula ($j_\ell=j_{\ell-1}s_\ell$, $r_\ell=r_{\ell-1}+(k_\ell-1)\delta_\ell j_{\ell-1}$), reaches **332 px** at the bottleneck (e5's last convolution); the complete layer-by-layer table (every stage, every jump and RF value) is in Appendix A. The trailing dilated pair's rates $(1,1,2,4)$ (the two rate-1 `double_conv` layers, then the explicit $\delta=2,4$ pair) satisfy the hybrid-dilated-convolution no-gridding condition of Wang et al. [Wang2018] — the full working of that check is in Appendix A; the leading rate-1 pair is load-bearing, not incidental, since a bare $(2,4,\dots)$ group sharing a common factor is exactly the configuration that condition flags as failing.

**Why conv-only fails the identity requirement.** Continuing the receptive-field recursion through a *conv-only* decoder path (the $\texttt{attn\_blocks}=0$ configuration, ablation A1, §5.4) reaches **364 px** (radius 182 px) at both the class head and the heatmap head — the two paths converge to the identical figure because every stage after the bottleneck is shared. This is the direct arithmetic behind contribution (1): identity inference from context needs a radius of roughly $0.85s$ for direct local identity and $1.85$–$2.85\,s$ for full context propagation, where $s$ is the apparent board-square size (§3.1). Tabulating both sides across the training envelope $s\in[16,128]$ px:

| $s$ (px) | direct-ID radius $0.85s$ | context radius $1.85s$–$2.85s$ | conv-only theoretical RF radius |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 13.6 | 29.6–45.6 | 182 |
| 32 | 27.2 | 59.2–91.2 | 182 |
| 64 | 54.4 | 118.4–182.4 | 182 (marginal at upper bound) |
| 128 | 108.8 | 236.8–364.8 | 182 (**fails**) |

Even the *theoretical* (best-case, ignoring effective-RF shrinkage) radius of 182 px stops covering the context-propagation band above $s\approx64$–98 px, and fails it outright by $s=128$ — and the *effective* receptive field is what actually matters: Luo et al.'s variance-addition argument [Luo2016] shows the effective RF standard deviation grows only as $O(\sqrt{n})$ with depth, so the usable ($1$–$2\sigma$) radius is a shrinking fraction of the theoretical figure as depth grows, further reduced by skip connections and ReLU. No amount of kernel re-weighting closes this gap (§2); the resolution this architecture adopts is to route around the conv path's ceiling entirely with global self-attention at the bottleneck, verified not merely argued by the effective-receptive-field probe (§5.2, metric M-07; ablation A1, §5.4).

**Tokenisation.** The bottleneck feature map $z\in\mathbb R^{B\times256\times H'\times W'}$ ($H'=H/16=75$, $W'=W/16=100$ at native res) is reshaped directly into $T=H'W'=7500$ tokens of dimension $d=256$ — a pure reshape, no learned projection:
$$
t = \texttt{flatten}(z, \text{dim}=2)^\top \in \mathbb R^{B\times T\times d}, \qquad \text{token index } n = \text{row}\cdot W' + \text{col} \ \ (\text{row-major})
$$
$d=256$ is chosen to equal the encoder's own channel width precisely so no patch-embedding convolution is needed — a documented deviation from TransUNet, whose hybrid mode still applies a learned $1\times1$ projection even over CNN features. Two further deviations from standard ViT/TransUNet practice: **no CLS token** (the task is dense per-position prediction, not image-level classification, so there is no global label for a CLS token to carry), and **no absolute positional embedding** (position is supplied purely through the relative-offset RoPE mechanism below, which matches the RoPE-ViT reference implementation's own default of no absolute PE — a departure from TransUNet specifically, not from the RoPE lineage). Token order is row-major (`col` varies fastest), matching both the reference RoPE-ViT implementation's own axial-index convention and the encoder's own `flatten` semantics — this correspondence is load-bearing for the axial split below to line up with the actual spatial grid.

**Attention blocks.** Two pre-norm transformer blocks (`n_blocks=2`), each:
$$
\tilde x = \mathrm{LN}_{\varepsilon=10^{-6}}(x), \qquad
q,k,v = \mathrm{RoPE}(W_Q\tilde x),\ \mathrm{RoPE}(W_K\tilde x),\ W_V\tilde x
$$
$$
x \leftarrow x + W_O\cdot\mathrm{SDPA}(q,k,v), \qquad
x \leftarrow x + W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}(W_1\,\mathrm{LN}_{\varepsilon=10^{-6}}(x))
$$
with $W_Q,W_K,W_V$ a single fused `nn.Linear(d,3d)`, $W_O$ a further `nn.Linear(d,d)`, and the MLP at ratio 4 ($d\to4d\to d$) — timm's `vision_transformer.Block` shape exactly, at its own non-default-overridden defaults (`qkv_bias=True`, no LayerScale, no DropPath, GELU activation). This is a pre-norm stack — Vaswani's original Transformer [Vaswani2017] is post-norm — a documented departure in favour of the cited timm/TransUNet reference implementations, both of which are pre-norm. RoPE is applied to $q,k$ only, never $v$: $v$ carries no positional information and is passed through unchanged, consistent with the derivation below, which rotates exactly the two tensors whose dot product must carry the relative-offset property. LayerNorm's $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ (rather than PyTorch's default $10^{-5}$) is set explicitly to match timm/TransUNet's own `encoder_norm`, and one further LayerNorm is applied to the token sequence after both blocks, before reshaping back to a spatial map — necessary because a pre-norm residual stack's output scale drifts additively with depth and nothing else re-normalises it before it is concatenated with the BN-conditioned decoder skip, matching TransUNet's own `encoder_norm` placement and timm's `final_norm=True` default. Per-block parameter accounting (qkv $197{,}376$ + proj $65{,}792$ + MLP up $263{,}168$ + MLP down $262{,}400$ + two LayerNorms $1{,}024$ = $789{,}760$/block) gives two blocks plus the final norm ($2d=512$): $2\times789{,}760+512=1{,}580{,}032\approx\mathbf{1.58\,M}$ parameters — matching the measured attention-block parameter count exactly (Appendix A) and confirming RoPE itself contributes zero learned parameters, since its `cos`/`sin` tables are precomputed constants (below).

Attention is computed via `F.scaled_dot_product_attention`, which auto-selects the flash kernel at this problem size (confirmed via profiler at $T=7500$, both bf16 training and fp16 deployment, with no memory-efficient/math fallback). Measured attention cost is **8.9% of forward time** (0.562 ms/block) — *lower* than its 13.8% share of total MACs (57.6 of 416.5 GMACs, Appendix A), because flash attention is bandwidth- rather than compute-bound at this problem size; materialising the full $(heads,T,T)$ attention matrix instead would cost tens of GiB per block direction at $T=7500$, which is why flash/memory-efficient SDPA is asserted, not merely preferred, at both training entry points.

**2D axial RoPE.** RoPE injects position by rotating $Q$ and $K$ before the dot product, rather than adding a position vector to the content [Su2021]. For a single frequency $\theta$ and a 2D pair $(x_1,x_2)$ at sequence position $m$:
$$
R(\theta m)\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}x_1\cos(\theta m) - x_2\sin(\theta m)\\ x_1\sin(\theta m) + x_2\cos(\theta m)\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q_m = R(\theta m)W_Q x_m$ and $k_n=R(\theta n)W_K x_n$, then since $R(\theta)^\top=R(-\theta)$ and $R(a)R(b)=R(a+b)$ for planar rotations,
$$
q_m^\top k_n = (W_Q x_m)^\top R(\theta m)^\top R(\theta n)\,(W_K x_n) = (W_Q x_m)^\top R\big(\theta(n-m)\big)\,(W_K x_n) = f\big(x_m, x_n,\ n-m\big)
$$
— the attention logit depends on content and the *relative* offset $n-m$ only, never on absolute position [Su2021, Eq. 13/16]. For the axial 2D case, position is a pair $(\text{row},\text{col})$, and the head dimension splits into disjoint pairs, half rotated by $\omega_i\cdot\text{row}$, half by $\omega_i\cdot\text{col}$; because disjoint dimension-pairs undergo independent planar rotations, the argument above applies pair-by-pair, giving $q_m^\top k_n = f(\text{content},\,\Delta\text{row},\,\Delta\text{col})$ — translation-equivariant by construction. This relative-geometry property is precisely why RoPE, rather than a learned absolute position table, is the right positional scheme for a task where identity is a relative inference ("which marker sits at which offset from this corner"); ablation A2 (§5.4) is designed to test this directly once run.

With $d=256$, $h=8$ heads, head dimension $d_h=d/h=32$; half of $d_h$ carries row phase, half carries column phase, giving $n=d_h/4=8$ frequency pairs per axis — matching RoPE-ViT's own axial definition [Heo2024]. The reference base-form frequency schedule, $\theta_i=b^{-i/n}$ for base $b$, cannot satisfy this system's own requirement that wavelengths span from $2$ token cells up to at least $2\times$ the token-grid extent: for *any* base $b$, the fastest frequency is $\theta_0=b^0=1$ rad/cell, giving a minimum wavelength of $2\pi\approx6.28$ cells regardless of $b$ — only the long end moves with base. This system instead anchors the spectrum directly in wavelengths, geometric between $\lambda_{\min}$ and $\lambda_{\max}$:
$$
\lambda_i = \lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\min}}\right)^{i/(n-1)}, \qquad \omega_i = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_i}, \qquad i=0,\dots,n-1,\ n=8,
$$
with $\lambda_{\min}=2.5$ cells (nudged up from the naive $2$ to avoid the Nyquist parity degeneracy at $\lambda=2$, where the rotation collapses to a bare sign-alternating parity bit) and $\lambda_{\max}=2\max(H',W')=200$ cells at native resolution — the choice that guarantees no two distinct in-grid token offsets ever produce an identical joint phase code (the no-global-alias proposition; full proof and its empirical check are in Appendix B). The resulting 8-frequency spectrum, shared across both axes and all 8 heads:

| $i$ | $\lambda_i$ (cells) | $\omega_i=2\pi/\lambda_i$ (rad/cell) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (fastest) | 2.500 | 2.5133 |
| 1 | 4.675 | 1.3439 |
| 2 | 8.743 | 0.7186 |
| 3 | 16.351 | 0.3843 |
| 4 | 30.579 | 0.2055 |
| 5 | 57.186 | 0.1099 |
| 6 | 106.945 | 0.0588 |
| 7 (slowest) | 200.000 | 0.0314 |

This is a deliberate, non-negotiable departure from the reference base-form parameterisation, not a re-tuning of it — no single base can move both spectrum ends independently, since the base-form's short end is pinned at $2\pi$ regardless of $b$. It sacrifices RoPE-ViT's cross-resolution frequency-extrapolation property: the trained checkpoint is valid only at its training input size and attend-stage, an explicit, accepted trade consistent with this system's static-shape export policy. Because ONNX has no complex tensor type, the implementation precomputes real `cos`/`sin` buffers (fp32, non-persistent — they carry zero learned content) and applies the rotate-half form directly, $\mathrm{RoPE}(x) = (x_1\cos\phi-x_2\sin\phi,\ x_1\sin\phi+x_2\cos\phi)$, pairing dimension $i$ with dimension $i+16$ (the GPT-NeoX/LLaMA-style layout) rather than RoFormer/rope-vit's adjacent-pair layout — the two are equivalent up to a fixed permutation of head dimensions for weights trained from scratch, as here.

**Attention gates.** Additive gates on the two coarsest skip connections (H/8, feeding d4; H/4, feeding d3), following Attention U-Net [Oktay2018]:
$$
\alpha(x,g) = \sigma_2\Big(\psi\big(\mathrm{ReLU}(W_x x + W_g g)\big)\Big)\ \text{(computed at the gating resolution, then bilinearly upsampled)}, \qquad y = x\cdot\alpha,
$$
where $W_x$ is a $1\times1$, stride-2, no-bias convolution on the skip (downsampling it to the gating signal's resolution), $W_g$ a $1\times1$ convolution (with bias) on the gating signal $g$, $\psi$ a $1\times1$ convolution to a single channel, and $\alpha$ is bilinearly upsampled (`align_corners=False`) back to the skip's spatial size and broadcast-multiplied across channels — a scalar per position, matching the paper's default single-channel $\alpha$. `gate4` gates the H/8 skip against the bottleneck output; `gate3` gates the H/4 skip against `d4`'s output. Pass-through initialisation is set by $\psi_w=0,\ \psi_b=3.0$, so
$$
\alpha_0 = \sigma\big(\psi(\mathrm{ReLU}(W_x x+W_g g))\big) = \sigma(3) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-3}} \approx 0.9526
$$
holds **exactly** for every position and every input at step 0 (the zero weight severs any input-dependence of $\alpha$ outright), while $\psi$'s weight still receives a nonzero gradient from the very first step — the gate is frozen in *output* at initialisation but not dead in *learning signal*. This strengthens the pass-through-init precedent traced to the official Attention U-Net repository's TORR variant (bias-only, $\psi_b=3.0$) by additionally zeroing the weight, making the gate exactly constant rather than merely biased toward constant. Writing the gated skip as $y=x\cdot\alpha(x,g)$, the gradient reaching the skip tensor is
$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = \alpha\cdot\uparrow \;+\; \frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial x}\cdot x\cdot\uparrow, \qquad \uparrow=\partial L/\partial y,
$$
which at initialisation ($\partial\alpha/\partial x\equiv0$ exactly) reduces to $\partial L/\partial x = 0.9526\cdot\uparrow$ — a pure scalar-scaled pass-through, 95% open. This is empirically fast-moving, not merely theoretically reversible: a 300-step sanity check on signal-free noise images measured gate statistics moving from mean/min $\approx0.9907$ at step 0 to mean $0.498$, min $0.317$ by step 300 — gates can and do close within a few hundred steps once a skip carries no useful signal, which is the expected optimum on noise but also the concrete confirmation that saturation-toward-closed is a real, fast-acting risk on real data if a skip's local evidence stops correlating with the label. This is why gate-$\alpha$ logging (mean/min per checkpoint) is mandatory from the first checkpoint onward, not an optional diagnostic.

Gates sit only on skips feeding the class-head tap (H/8, H/4); the full-resolution and H/2 skips (feeding only the heatmap head) stay permanently ungated. The criterion is an asymmetric-risk argument: gate only where the downstream lattice-recovery machinery (§3.6) can undo a wrong veto. A corner whose local identity evidence is suppressed by a closed gate falls back to identity-less and can still be recovered by the lattice-projection pass; a corner whose *heatmap* response is vetoed by a closed gate is gone with no recovery path at all — hence the heatmap-only skips must never be gated. A second argument is a blur-energy one: under this system's 3–7 px motion/Gaussian blur range (§4.2), degraded junctions carry their remaining energy specifically at the H/2 scale, so gating H/2 risks suppressing exactly the signal a blurred junction still has. Gate scope is a named ablation axis, not dogma: `gate_all_skips: true` additionally gates the H/2 and full-resolution skips at the same pass-through init, adopted only if ablation A3 (§5.4) shows it matches recall on the darkest brightness bins and smallest $s$-octave *and* costs nothing on degraded real captures — a two-sided test, not a foregone conclusion. The point-by-point comparison against the official reference implementation's own gate variant is in Appendix A.

**Decoder & heads.** One shared decoder, bilinear-upsample ($\times2$, `align_corners=False`) + channel-concat + a single $3\times3$ `conv_bn_relu` per rung — never a transposed convolution:

| Rung | Concat inputs | Channel arithmetic | Skip gating |
|---|---|---|---|
| d4 (H/16→H/8) | `up2(z)` (256) ⊕ `gate4(s4,z)` (256) | $512\to256$ | gated |
| d3 (H/8→H/4) | `up2(d4)` (256) ⊕ `gate3(s3,d4)` (128) | $384\to128$ | gated |
| d2 (H/4→H/2) | `up2(d3)` (128) ⊕ `s2` (64) | $192\to64$ | **ungated** |
| d1 (H/2→H) | `up2(d2)` (64) ⊕ `s1` (32) | $96\to32$ | **ungated** |

The class head reads `d3`'s output directly (H/4); the heatmap ladder continues through d2, d1 to full resolution — class-loss gradients stop at the d3 tap, invisible to `d2`, `d1`, and the heatmap head entirely. Both heads are a $3\times3$ conv, ReLU, then a $1\times1$ projection to raw logits (no sigmoid inside the network; sigmoid is applied only in the loss, §3.4, and the inference pipeline, §3.6):
$$
\text{hm}(y) = \mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}\big(\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times3,32\to32}(y))\big)\!:32\to1 \qquad
\text{cls}(y) = \mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}\big(\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times3,128\to128}(y))\big)\!:128\to16.
$$
**Bias initialisation** $b=-2.19$ on both heads' final $1\times1$ conv: $b=\ln(\pi/(1-\pi))$ with $\pi=0.1$, so $\sigma(b)=\pi=0.1$ at step 0 — every logit starts predicting a modest 10% positive probability rather than the 50% a zero-init would give over $\sim1.9$M mostly-background pixels, which would otherwise inflate the initial loss by orders of magnitude and destabilise early training. This diverges from the $\alpha{=}2/\beta{=}4$ focal-loss lineage's usual $\pi=0.01$ [Law2018; Lin2017]: that prior was calibrated for $\sim$80 sparse COCO object classes per image, whereas this system has up to 16 *dense* corner positives per image, so a less extreme positive-suppression prior is the correct degeneration. **Transposed-conv ban.** Bilinear-upsample-then-conv is used everywhere; transposed convolutions are prohibited on the grounds that uneven kernel/stride overlap produces checkerboard-frequency artefacts [Odena2016] that would beat directly against this system's own checkerboard-patterned target — every upsample here is immediately followed by a conv and a concatenation with a full-resolution skip, so high-frequency content re-enters from the skip rather than needing to be synthesised by the upsample.

**Compute budget and attention placement.** Self-attention over $T$ tokens costs $O(T^2)$; this system's scaling rule is to attend at the deepest encoder stage with at most $\approx5{,}000$ tokens, moving to a coarser stage as input grows. At native $1600\times1200$, $H/16=100\times75=7{,}500$ tokens (the nearest legal grid; $H/32$ is not integral). Had attention instead stayed at $H/8$ for this input ($T=30{,}000$), SDPA alone would cost $2\times(2\times30000^2\times256)=\mathbf{921.6}$ GMACs — more than *double* the entire actual network's total compute — which is the concrete, measured reason the H/16 rule is architectural, not a soft preference. The resulting production network — five convolutional encoder stages, a two-block axial-RoPE attention bottleneck at H/16, gated skips at both H/8 and H/4, and a decoder with the added rung above — measures **7.12 M parameters** ($7{,}124{,}659$, against a $7{,}124{,}700$ design target, $0.001\%$ relative error) and **12.69 ms** median forward latency at batch 1, fp16 ($78.8$ fps, $65.7$ TFLOPS achieved against $416.5$ GMACs $=833.1$ GFLOPs of analytic compute). This is a $4.7\times$ latency cost for $6.25\times$ the pixels of a smaller $640\times480$ variant used for cheaper architecture ablations (§5.4) — sub-linear, precisely because the $H/16$ attention rule keeps token growth to $1.56\times$ rather than the $6.25\times$ it would otherwise be. The complete per-stage parameter table, the full GMACs breakdown, and the $640\times480$-vs-native comparison are in Appendix A.

### 3.4 Supervision: Gaussian targets & penalty-reduced focal losses

Targets are rendered in the dataloader directly from the analytic corner labels the synthetic data engine produces (§4), never persisted to disk. The shared convention across both heads is an **unnormalised** Gaussian splat, combined across corners by **per-pixel maximum** (never sum), with the exact containing pixel/cell then **forced to 1.0**.

**Heatmap target.** For each visible corner $k$ at continuous position $p_k=(x_k,y_k)$:
$$
Y(x,y) = \max_k\ \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert (x,y)-p_k\rVert^2}{2\sigma_{hm}^2}\right), \qquad \sigma_{hm}=2\text{ px},
$$
computed only inside a $\pm3\sigma$ window per corner, followed by an explicit override: `hm[round(p_{k,y}), round(p_{k,x})] = 1.0` for every visible corner. **This override is load-bearing, not cosmetic.** At the worst-case half-pixel phase (a corner centred exactly between four pixels), the analytic Gaussian value at the nearest pixel is $\exp(-0.5/(2\cdot2^2))=\exp(-0.0625)=0.9394<1$. Since the focal loss's positive branch is gated by *exact* equality ($Y=1$), without the forced override that branch would never fire for such a corner — it would instead be routed through the negative branch, and the entire positive learning signal would vanish from training for that corner. This is CenterNet's own convention, verbatim: *"If two Gaussians of the same class overlap, we take the element-wise maximum"* [Zhou2019] — the max-combine rule is grounded exactly; the forced-1.0 override closes the gap the continuous Gaussian alone leaves open.

**Class targets (H/4).** Sixteen independent per-index channels, rendered at $H/4\times W/4$ with $\sigma_{cls}=1$ **cell**:
$$
Y_k(u,v) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(u-x_c)^2+(v-y_c)^2}{2\sigma_{cls}^2}\right), \qquad x_c = \frac{x+0.5}{4}-0.5,\quad y_c=\frac{y+0.5}{4}-0.5,
$$
with the containing cell forced to 1.0. The half-pixel cell-coordinate map $x_c=(x{+}0.5)/4-0.5$ is the same pixel-centre convention as §3.2's resolution map; solving $x_c=j$ for $x$ gives $x=4j+1.5$, the geometric centre of the 4-pixel input span cell $j$ aggregates, confirming the mapping is self-consistent under downsampling. The worst-case forced-peak gap here is larger than the heatmap's: at cell-space offset $(0.5,0.5)$ with $\sigma_{cls}=1$, the unforced value is $\exp(-0.5/2)=0.7788<1$ — again closed only by the explicit override.

**Focal loss (both branches).** CornerNet's penalty-reduced focal form [Law2018], implemented in **logit space**:
$$
L = -\sum_{x,y}\begin{cases}(1-\hat p)^\alpha\log\hat p & Y=1\\ (1-Y)^\beta\,\hat p^\alpha\log(1-\hat p) & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}, \qquad \alpha=2,\ \beta=4,\ \hat p=\sigma(z),
$$
computed from raw logits $z$ via `logsigmoid`, not from a clamped sigmoid. **Why logit space, not a sigmoid-clamp.** The reference CornerNet/CenterNet implementations instead clamp the sigmoid output (`clamp(sigmoid(x), 1e-4, 1-1e-4)`) before taking logs. Under bf16 (7 explicit mantissa bits), a sigmoid output for a sufficiently large logit rounds to *exactly* $1.0$ in bf16 representation — at that point a naive $\log(1-\hat p)$ evaluates $\log(0)=-\infty$, and multiplying by the nonzero penalty weight propagates $-\infty\times\text{finite}=-\infty$ with a $0\times\infty$ indeterminate gradient — NaN. Every visible corner's forced $Y{=}1$ peak sits adjacent to cells whose $Y$ is small but nonzero, which is precisely where this failure mode is most likely to fire during bf16-autocast training; computing $\log\hat p$/$\log(1-\hat p)$ directly via `logsigmoid`'s numerically stable form avoids ever forming $\hat p$ itself near the boundary.

**Normaliser.** $N=\max\big(\sum_{\text{batch}}(\text{visible corners}),\,1\big)$, a single scalar shared by **both** heads, clamped $\ge1$ so all-negative batches or fully-occluded positives never divide by zero. This is CornerNet's own *code* convention rather than its paper prose: the paper states $N=$ "number of objects in an image" (per-image), but the reference implementation sums positives over the entire *batch* tensor, and CenterNet's own `num_pos` sums over all class channels *jointly*, never per-channel — this system's shared-$N$, batch-summed reading matches both reference codebases.

**Shared-$N$ argument, with a worked example.** Each of the 16 class channels has *at most one* positive per image (each corner index appears at most once); a **per-channel** $N_k\in\{0,1\}$ (clamped $\ge1$, per channel) would degenerate to no normalisation at all. Concretely: for a training image with 10 of 16 corners visible, the shared-$N$ divisor is $N=10$ for every term of both $L_{hm}$ and $L_{cls}$. A per-channel divisor would instead give $N_k=1$ for *every one* of the 16 channels — the 10 channels with a real positive clamp to 1 because that is their actual (correct) positive count, but the 6 channels with *no* corner in this image *also* clamp to 1 rather than sharing in the image's true 10-corner scale. The net effect is that every one of the 16 channels' loss terms divides by 1 instead of by the image's actual visible-corner count — a uniform $10\times$ inflation of $L_{cls}$ relative to $L_{hm}$ for this example image, growing to the full $16\times$ figure at the (also legal) 16-visible-corner extreme. This inflation is silent — nothing crashes, no NaN, the loss simply trains at the wrong relative scale.

**Element-count calibration of $\lambda_{cls}=1$.** At native resolution ($H\times W=1200\times1600$): the heatmap has $1200\times1600\times1=1{,}920{,}000$ output elements; the class map has $(1200/4)\times(1600/4)\times16=300\times400\times16=1{,}920{,}000$ — **exactly equal**. This is not a coincidence of one resolution: since $(H/4)(W/4)(16)\equiv HW$ identically for any $H,W$ divisible by 4, the two heads' element counts (and hence their background-gradient masses at $\lambda_{cls}=1$) are equal by construction of the H/4-with-16-channels design, at *any* input size.

**The partial-reward argument (contribution 2).** The class head's loss is 16 *independent* penalty-reduced focal terms rather than a single 17-way softmax-with-dustbin (Deep ChArUco's own ID head, following SuperPoint's convention, §2) or a plain, non-penalty-reduced BCE. This is a direct, deliberate response to a training-formulation problem the whole ChArUcoNet/SuperPoint/RefineNet lineage shares: a one-hot cross-entropy target — SuperPoint's 65-way cell softmax, RefineNet's 4096-way sub-pixel softmax — treats every incorrect answer identically, so a prediction one cell from the truth and a prediction on the opposite side of the image cost the same loss. The penalty-reduced Gaussian-focal form instead grants **graded partial reward inside the target disc**: the $(1-Y)^\beta$ term is near-zero close to the peak and rises toward full penalty only away from it, so a near-miss costs measurably less than a far-miss — ordinal structure a one-hot target cannot express at all. This asymmetry cuts the other way for identity, too, and is what a single scalar-weighted or softmax loss cannot express jointly: the *correct* channel should be forgiving near the corner (a Gaussian disc of near-zero penalty, so the network may saturate confidence across the disc rather than trace the target's exact shape — the property §3.6's bilinear readout depends on for off-centre-phase confidence), while *every wrong* channel must carry full penalty everywhere, including inside the correct channel's own disc. A softmax necessarily couples all 16 channels' probabilities to sum to 1, so it cannot simultaneously let the correct channel saturate *and* hold every wrong channel at zero over the same spatial disc — probability mass "forgiven" on the correct channel is mass necessarily redistributed elsewhere. This per-channel independence is also what makes the *permissive-not-prescriptive* supervision of §3.1 possible at all: because no channel is forced to compete against the others for probability mass, "heatmap high, every class channel low" is a state the loss can reward honestly rather than one only reachable by construction. Ablation A10 (§5.4) is designed to test this directly: a $\beta=0$ variant (plain focal BCE) is predicted to produce noisier class maps near corners; a 17-way softmax variant is predicted to produce confidence that entangles with distance-to-corner rather than staying flat across the disc, exposed directly by the $p_{\text{id}}$-vs-sub-cell-phase instrumentation carried by metric M-04 (§5.2).

### 3.5 Sub-pixel refinement

**Refiner architecture.** `Refiner` is a separate, board-agnostic network: a $1\times24\times24$ sensor-resolution crop in, a $1\times64\times64$ logit map out (central $8\times8$ px at $8\times$ resolution):
$$
f = \big[\texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(1,32)\to\texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(32,64)\to\texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(64,64)\big](x)\Big|_{[8{:}16,\,8{:}16]} \ \to\ \texttt{conv\_bn\_relu}(64,64) \ \to\ \mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}(64\to64;\ b=-2.19)\ \to\ \mathrm{PixelShuffle}(8).
$$
Three conv-BN-ReLU stages run at the full $24\times24$; the feature map is then **centre-cropped** to the central $8\times8$ — the only region the target ever populates, since the $\pm4$ px capture range sits entirely inside it — before a fourth conv stage and a $1\times1\to64$-channel logit projection, PixelShuffled by $8\times$ into the $64\times64$ output. The "never upsample the input" principle is honoured structurally: every upsampling operation acts on already-computed features, never on the raw $24\times24$ input. Measured parameter count is **97,056** (target 96,600, $0.47\%$ relative error). Applying the same receptive-field recursion as §3.3 to the refiner's four $3\times3$ convolutions (no pooling, so jump stays 1 throughout) gives $r_0{=}1\to3\to5\to7\to$ **9 px** after the final body convolution — tight against this system's 3–7 px motion/Gaussian-blur augmentation range (§4.2); if refiner validation error (metric M-03, §5.2) is found to correlate with blur severity, the documented, zero-extra-parameter remedy is to dilate the body convolutions to rate 2 (RF 13 px) rather than adding depth. This is a documented departure from Deep ChArUco's own RefineNet, whose mechanism is an FC-bottleneck 4096-way softmax classification head over the enlarged region [Hu2019, §3.2]; this system instead uses a fully convolutional PixelShuffle head with the same focal-BCE loss as the detector and a windowed soft-argmax readout (below) — a departure on mechanism, target, loss, and readout together, made for architectural consistency (one loss form across every head) and export cleanliness (no FC bottleneck to fold, TensorRT-clean). The unofficial reference implementation independently reaches the same conclusion about the FC bottleneck: its own `RefineNet` is also fully convolutional (three stacked nearest-neighbour upsamples, no FC bottleneck), diverging from its own paper on exactly this point.

**Refiner target and the reject-resample jitter policy.** The refiner's target is an unnormalised Gaussian rendered over the full $64\times64$ output canvas, centred at
$$
u^* = 31.5 + 8\,d_x, \qquad v^* = 31.5 + 8\,d_y, \qquad Y_{\text{ref}}(u,v) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(u-u^*)^2+(v-v^*)^2}{2\times1.5^2}\right),
$$
with the same forced-1.0 override at the rounded peak, trained with the identical focal form of §3.4 (normalised by batch size $B$ rather than a visible-corner count, since each crop carries exactly one forced-1.0 positive by construction). An explicit support-bound assertion enforces $\max(|d_x|,|d_y|)\le3.9375$ px: at $8\times$ magnification the $64\times64$ canvas covers $[0,63]$ output pixels symmetric about centre index $31.5$, so the largest representable offset in either direction is exactly $(63-31.5)/8=3.9375$ px. The refiner training/validation stream samples jitter $j\sim U(-4,+4)^2$ per axis — matching the $\pm4$ px capture range — which can and does occasionally exceed this $\pm3.9375$ px representable support; rather than clamping such draws (biasing the offset distribution's tails toward the clamp boundary) or accepting them (crashing the target-renderer's own assertion), the generator resamples up to 3 times and skips the corner entirely if none of the 3 tries land in-support (an incidence of roughly 5%). Even a resampled-into-support offset near the boundary sits close enough to the canvas edge that the analytic Gaussian target itself is asymmetrically clipped, independent of the soft-argmax window's own truncation discussed next.

**Peaks and deduplication.** Stage-1 output feeds the refiner through a fixed decode: a $3\times3$ max-pool-equality peak rule at threshold $\tau_{hm}=0.3$, capped at top-$K=64$ — CenterNet's own decode rule verbatim (`keep = (hm == maxpool(hm)) & (hm >= τ_hm)`) — followed by greedy nearest-neighbour deduplication in descending-score order, merge radius 2 px, with a deterministic lexicographic tiebreak on exact plateau ties. The deduplication step has no analogue in the CenterNet reference decode; it is this system's own addition, motivated by the reference peak rule's own known behaviour of returning separate peaks on an exact plateau.

**Crop cutting.** Each kept peak's $24\times24$ sensor-frame crop is cut at
$$
c = \mathrm{round}\!\left(\frac{p_{\text{input}}+0.5}{r}-0.5\right), \qquad \text{crop} = \text{frame}_{\text{sensor}}[c_y{-}12:c_y{+}12,\ c_x{-}12:c_x{+}12],
$$
the same half-pixel resolution map as §3.2, with $r$ the sensor/input downsample ratio ($r=1$ at native resolution). A peak whose crop would cross the sensor border is **excluded** and bypasses Stage 2 entirely, retaining its coarse (unrefined) coordinate — never reflect-padded, because reflect-padding a border crop manufactures a fake mirrored saddle structure at the pad seam that the refiner has never been trained to distinguish from a genuine corner, actively injecting a plausible-looking false signal rather than simply degrading gracefully.

**Soft-argmax readout and its truncation-bias floor.**
$$
u^*,v^* = \sum_{(i,j)\in W_{5\times5}(\hat a)} (i,j)\cdot\frac{\hat p(i,j)}{\sum_{W_{5\times5}}\hat p}, \qquad xy_{\text{refined}} = c + \frac{(u^*,v^*)-31.5}{8},
$$
where $\hat a$ is the hard argmax and the $5\times5$ window is clamped so its top-left stays in-bounds. This is not Sun et al.'s integral-regression estimator [Sun2018] despite the shared "expectation readout" idea: Sun's is a full-map softmax-normalised expectation with no windowing at all; the $5\times5$ restriction is this project's own design, guarded operationally by the refiner content check (§4.7). The window is an instrument property with a measured, non-zero floor even for a perfect network: a $5\times5$ window against the refiner's actual $\sigma=1.5$ target covers only $\approx1.33\sigma$, giving a worst-case forced-peak readout error of **0.1876** grid units ($\approx0.024$ sensor px) against a bound of 0.35, rising to $\approx0.69$ grid units ($\approx0.086$ sensor px) at the outer $\approx0.4$ px sliver of the $\pm3.9375$ px support, where the analytic target itself is truncated by the finite canvas edge. Both figures are readout-side floors that exist even with a perfectly-trained network and should not be chased as training deficiencies when validation tails are examined near the support boundary.

### 3.6 Geometric correspondence, consistency gating & pose

`detect()` orchestrates Stage 3 over three coordinate spaces — **input** (network resolution), **sensor** (native frame, ratio $r=$ input/sensor, $r=1$ at native res), and **pinhole** (undistorted sensor space, where gate/recovery/PnP geometry runs) — connected by one half-pixel convention throughout (§3.2). `detect()` never imports the detector module itself; a trained model and refiner arrive pre-built and are only ever called, so every function in this stage stays importable and unit-testable independent of the network's own availability or state.

**Bilinear ID readout.** The refined coordinate is used to sample the class map by `grid_sample` at
$$
g_x = \frac{2(x_{\text{input}}+0.5)}{4W_4}-1, \qquad g_y = \frac{2(y_{\text{input}}+0.5)}{4H_4}-1, \qquad
\text{sampled} = \mathrm{grid\_sample}(\text{cls},\ (g_x,g_y);\ \text{bilinear, border, align\_corners=False}),
$$
where $4W_4=W$ is the class map's implied input width; the channel-wise maximum gives $(\text{index},\text{confidence})$ — a per-channel value, not a calibrated posterior. This formula is exactly, not approximately, the training target's own convention: substituting the class-target cell-coordinate definition of §3.4 into the standard `align_corners=False` normalised-coordinate formula reproduces $g_x$ above algebraically with no explicit $\div4$ step — the full symbolic derivation is in Appendix B. Measured worst-case confidence at the analytically worst phase (a corner sitting exactly on a shared 4-cell boundary, splitting bilinear weight four ways) is **0.8355** (random-phase sweep) / **0.8341** (deterministic worst phase), both comfortably above an asserted floor of 0.24; at cell-centre phase, confidence reads back **0.999995**.

**Undistortion.** `cv2.undistortPoints(xy_sensor, K, dist, P=K)` maps refined corners to pinhole space; all downstream geometry (gate, recovery, PnP) runs there exclusively, since a homography is a valid model of a planar board only in undistorted coordinates. This step is non-negotiable, quantitatively: for a modest, realistic lens ($k_1\approx-0.08$, $f\approx550$ px), radial distortion magnitude scales as $\Delta\approx k_1r^3/f^2$ — at $r=250$ px, $\Delta\approx-4.13$ px; at a $640\times480$ frame's half-diagonal ($r\approx400$ px), $\Delta\approx-16.9$ px. Both figures exceed the lattice gate's own 3 px reprojection tolerance well before the frame edge; an ungated-for-distortion pipeline would demote true identities increasingly often moving outward from the image centre, mistaking a systematic lens effect for detection noise. High-distortion or fisheye lenses are handled by rectifying the entire frame before Stage 1, since the training distribution itself is pinhole; native fisheye operation is out of scope unless the synthetic data engine (§4) gains its own distortion model.

**Lattice-consistency gate.** A RANSAC-fit homography from the unitless canonical lattice ($(\text{col}{+}1,\text{row}{+}1)$ for index $i=4\cdot\text{row}{+}\text{col}$) to the ID'd detections' image coordinates, tolerance 3 px, with guards applied in order: (1) **too few** — fewer than 4 ID'd corners, no fit; (2) **collinearity** — the minimum singular value of the ID'd canonical points' centred coordinates below $10^{-6}$, no fit (a clipped board showing one lattice row is exactly this case: a $4\times4$ grid has 14 lines with $\ge3$ collinear points, a realistic, not merely theoretical, failure mode); (3) **vacuity** — exactly 4 ID'd, non-collinear corners fit *exactly* (zero residual), so a hallucinated identity passes the RANSAC test with no internal redundancy to reject on at all; the fit is flagged `degenerate='vacuous'` and not trusted for PnP unless corroborated; (4) **$\ge5$ ID'd** — the gate functions as intended, demoting dissenting identities to identity-less and fixing indexing on the maximal consistent subset. This gate is the mechanism behind contribution (3): every learned per-corner identity is independently re-checked against a single rigid board layout, converting a wrong identity into a detected, demoted outlier rather than a silent pose error.

**Recovery.** Every one of the 16 canonical corners is projected through the fitted homography; an identity-less detection within tolerance of a projected corner inherits that index, with each canonical index claimed at most once (a greedy, nearest-available match in detection order). A vacuous (exactly-4) fit is corroborated only if at least one recovery lands. Measured on a synthetic pose test with exact corner localisation, the recovery/PnP geometry chain is exact to floating-point precision (rotation error $1.6\times10^{-13}$°, translation error $2.55\times10^{-14}\%$ of board diagonal), isolating any real-world error to the detection/refinement stages rather than the geometric machinery itself.

**IPPE PnP.** `cv2.solvePnPGeneric(..., SOLVEPNP_IPPE, distCoeffs=None)` on the undistorted, ID'd subset — OpenCV's implementation of Collins and Bartoli's infinitesimal plane-based pose algorithm [Collins2014]. OpenCV's IPPE solver always returns two planar solutions, sorted ascending by reprojection error; the pipeline computes the ambiguity ratio $\text{err}_2/\text{err}_1<1.5$ order-agnostically (min/max of the two returned errors) rather than trusting that ordering, since the sorting guarantee is confirmed at the source level but not documented for this entry point. At exact fronto-parallel viewing, noiseless, the two IPPE solutions coincide to $\sim4\times10^{-8}$ — the textbook two-fold planar degeneracy; realistic detector-scale noise ($0.15$ px, comparable to the refiner's own measured error) is what makes the two reprojection errors nonzero and genuinely comparable, so the ambiguity flag is a property of the noise draw at near-fronto-parallel geometry, not of a specific small tilt angle. **Empty-result no-pose.** OpenCV's IPPE call is wrapped internally in a bare `catch(...)`: an internal solver failure is silently swallowed and zero solutions returned, confirmed at the source level. `detect()` treats an empty result as a defined `'pnp_solver_failed'` outcome rather than an unhandled exception.

**Output.** `detect()` returns, per frame: `rvec, tvec, rms, reason` (`None` on success), `ambiguous`, `demoted`, `recovered` counts, and the `corners` list of §3.1's schema, with corner coordinates reported in **sensor** space (post-refinement, pre-undistort) — the natural frame for drawing detections back onto the captured image; pinhole coordinates remain an internal representation used only for gate/recovery/PnP geometry. A rising `recovered` fraction is the documented early-warning signal that the class head is operating out of its training domain. A worked symbolic trace of one corner through every coordinate transition in this section, with small illustrative numbers chosen purely to exercise the arithmetic, is in Appendix B.

## 4. Synthetic Training Data

Training is $100\%$ synthetic: no real image is ever labelled, and every sample the detector or refiner sees is generated on the fly, on demand, by one canonical generation function per stream (`dcc/synth.py`). This section gives the full compositing order, every sampled parameter, the label schema, the determinism discipline, and the measured acceptance-audit numbers that gate the corpus before any training run; the exhaustive per-parameter config tables and the optics/scale envelope equation are in Appendix C.

### 4.1 Compositing pipeline

Per positive sample, in this exact order:

1. **Negative decision.** A coin-flip at probability $\texttt{negative\_p}=0.05$ decides whether this sample has a board at all (§4.5); everything below runs conditionally on a positive draw except where noted.
2. **Background preparation.** A background image is sampled (sorted file list — §4.6 — from a COCO-formatted corpus or an equivalent image directory), horizontally flipped with probability $0.5$, rotated uniformly in $[-180°, 180°]$ about its own centre (reflect-bordered), then padded (reflect) and randomly cropped to the network's input canvas.
3. **Board render, warp, and paste** *(positive samples only)*. The exact locked board (§3.2) is rendered fresh at $480\times480$ ($SQ=96$ px); a $6$-DoF affine placement composed with a tilt-calibrated perspective factor (§4.3) is sampled; the rendered board is histogram-matched to the background crop (`skimage.exposure.match_histograms`) and warped in by full $3\times3$ homography with alpha compositing.
4. **Occlusion**, gated by one shared flag covering two mechanisms (§4.4): an offline-built bank of real-object cutouts is alpha-composited in first (so later rectangular dropout holes can, and do, land on top of a placed object exactly as they would on a real occluder), followed by rectangular dropout holes.
5. **Visibility.** A purely geometric point-in-region test (§4.4) marks each of the sixteen corners visible or not, given the frame boundary and every occluding region placed in step 4.
6. **Photometric augmentation** (§4.2), applied to the whole composite in a fixed float32 workspace, clipped to `uint8` only once at the very end.
7. **Grayscale conversion**, last.

Negative samples run steps 2, 4, 6, 7 only (no board, no per-corner visibility — there are no corners); this is deliberate, since it keeps the class channels honest at zero and the heatmap honest on genuinely empty scenes.

The **affine placement** samples isotropic scale $s=\exp(U(\ln16,\ln128))$ (log-uniform, this *is* the recorded $s_{\text{px}}$ — §3.1's operating envelope), in-plane rotation $\theta\sim U(-\pi,\pi)$, independent shear angles $\pm35°$ per axis, and translation $\pm45\%$ of the canvas per axis; partial-view placements (board clipping the frame at large $s$) and zero-visible-corner positives fall out of this sampling naturally and are legal samples, not filtered — this is precisely the "no partial-view close-range operation" gap of §1 being trained for directly.

### 4.2 Photometric model

Applied to the composite, float32 workspace, each independently gated by its own probability (config values as shipped):

| Augmentation | Parameters | $p$ |
|---|---|---|
| Gaussian noise | $\sigma \sim U(3.2, 7.1)$ | 0.5 |
| Motion blur | line kernel, $k\in\{3,5\}$, random angle | 0.5 |
| Gaussian blur | $k\in\{3,5,7\}$ | 0.25 |
| Multiplicative field | $U(0.95, 1.05)$ per pixel | 0.5 |
| Brightness (mixed semantics, below) | $b\sim U(-0.8, 0.35)$, multiplicative or additive-by-max, coin-flipped | 0.5 |
| RGB shift | $\pm 20$ per channel | 0.5 |
| Soft glare (below) | elliptical Gaussian falloff, additive | 0.3 |
| Ghosting (below) | $\alpha\sim U(0.1,0.5)$, shift $\sim U(1,4)$ px | 0.25 |

Three of these — mixed brightness semantics, soft glare, and ghosting — extend the reference augmentation lineage's list beyond generic darkness and blur; the deployment scenario that motivates them (an active-illumination lit/dark differencing camera, §6) is described in full there, and only the mechanism is given here. **Mixed brightness semantics.** Each application coin-flips between multiplicative ($\text{img}\cdot(1{+}b)$) and additive-by-max ($\text{img}+b\cdot255$) scaling, spanning both a photon-starved regime (multiplicative darkening) and a clipped-black regime (additive) rather than only one. **Soft glare.** An additive elliptical Gaussian-falloff patch, clipped at the sensor's saturation value — glare-saturated pixels are dead zones (zero information after subtraction) in the deployment domain this augments for, not bright regions; the dead-zone consequence of saturation is separately modelled by the dropout-hole mechanism's white/black fill values, which do gate visibility (§4.4), whereas glare itself is photometric only and never touches the geometric ground truth. **Ghosting.** A motion-between-frames residual,
$$
\text{img}' = \text{clip}\big((1+a)\cdot\text{img} - a\cdot\text{shift\_blur}(\text{img}, \delta)\big),\qquad a\sim U(0.1, 0.5),
$$
where $\text{shift\_blur}$ is a small Gaussian blur followed by a rigid shift of magnitude $\delta\sim U(1,4)$ px in a random direction — modelling the double-edge residual that surviving scene motion between an illuminator-on and illuminator-off exposure pair leaves after subtraction, concentrated exactly at the square boundaries that carry corner evidence, which a generic blur augmentation does not reproduce. Every one of these three additions is config-gated independently of the rectangular occlusion/cutout gate, so the corpus can be ablated back to the literal reference augmentation list at will (ablation A7, §5.4) to test whether they matter on real degraded captures once they exist.

### 4.3 Tilt-calibrated homography sampling

The 6-DoF affine placement alone contains no foreshortening — an affine map cannot represent a physically tilted board — and a detector trained only on affine warps meets every genuinely tilted board out of distribution. The generator therefore composes the affine map $M$ (lifted to a $3\times3$ matrix) with a small projective factor $P$, applied about the render's own centre $c_r$ so that $P(c_r)$ dehomogenises to exactly $c_r$:
$$
P = T_{c_r}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\g_x&g_y&1\end{pmatrix}T_{-c_r},\qquad
g = \frac{\sin(\tau_{\text{tilt}})\cdot s}{f\cdot SQ},\qquad
(g_x, g_y) = g\cdot(\cos\psi,\ \sin\psi),
$$
with $\tau_{\text{tilt}}\sim U(0,60°)$ (drawn, but only *kept* — otherwise forced to zero, a pure affine warp — with probability $\texttt{perspective\_p}=0.8$), $\psi\sim U(0,2\pi)$, and virtual focal length $f=\texttt{fov\_scale}\cdot W$, $\texttt{fov\_scale}\sim U(0.7,1.4)$. This is calibrated to match, to first order in $\tau_{\text{tilt}}$, the homography a genuinely tilted fronto-parallel board of apparent scale $s$ would induce in front of a virtual pinhole camera of focal length $f$; the full first-order derivation, and the documented subtlety that $\psi$ is the foreshortening-gradient axis rather than the physical Rodrigues tilt axis (the two are perpendicular), are in Appendix B. The calibration was verified numerically against an independently constructed pinhole model, agreeing to machine precision ($\le10^{-9}$ relative) at the exact render centre and across the full tilt range:

| $\tau_{\text{tilt}}$ | Relative error vs. independent pinhole model |
|---|---|
| $1°$ | $\approx 0$ |
| $5°$ | $0.0006$ |
| $15°$ | $0.006$ |
| $30°$ | $0.026$ |
| $45°$ | $0.067$ |
| $60°$ | $0.142$ |

— comfortably inside a $\le0.02$ gate at $\tau_{\text{tilt}}\le5°$ and a $\le0.15$ gate at $\tau_{\text{tilt}}\le60°$, confirming the first-order approximation is tight where the majority of training mass sits and degrades gracefully toward the $60°$ envelope edge. As a stream-alignment discipline matching the rest of the generator, the perspective coin and all three of its draws ($\tau_{\text{tilt}}$, $\psi$, $\texttt{fov\_scale}$) always consume exactly four random-generator calls in a fixed order regardless of the coin's outcome, so changing `perspective_p` or the tilt range never desynchronises any other sampler's draw sequence for a fixed seed.

### 4.4 Occlusion and labelling policy

**Rectangular dropout.** With probability $0.4$, between one and six rectangular holes ($16$–$64$ px per side) are punched into the composite, filled with one of $\{0, 128, 255, \text{noise}\}$.

**Object-cutout occlusion.** A second, additive occlusion mechanism models real clutter sitting in front of the board rather than dead sensor zones or dropout: an offline pass builds a bank of segmented real-object cutouts; at generation time, up to three cutouts per sample are independently drawn, resized, rotated, alpha-feathered, and composited into the scene, with their placed alpha accumulated (by maximum) into an occlusion mask. The bank is built once, ahead of time, by `tools/gen_cutouts.py` under a separate environment carrying the segmentation model: per COCO image, an automatic mask generator built on Segment Anything Model 2 [Ravi2024] proposes candidate object masks, each kept only if its area fraction falls in $[0.5\%,25\%]$, its predicted IoU is $\ge0.85$ and stability score $\ge0.9$, it clears the image border by at least $2$ px on every side (dropping artificially straight border-clipped edges), and its solidity — mask pixel area divided by convex-hull area — is $\ge0.4$ (dropping wire-frame or hollow mask artifacts); surviving masks are cropped to their bounding box and saved as RGBA cutouts, one file per object, alongside a manifest recording the exact filter parameters and a content hash of the resulting file list.

**Cutout bank status.** The bank is complete. Following an initial 30-image smoke sweep (126 cutouts, with the alpha-visibility interaction independently confirmed — a synthetic scene with ten corners placed under a cutout correctly marked all ten invisible), the full production sweep over $3{,}000$ images of this project's COCO corpus has run to completion: the bank directory holds **14,721** RGBA cutout files (averaging $\approx4.9$ objects per swept image), and `manifest.json` is present and fully self-consistent with the directory contents (every one of its 14,721 listed files verified present on disk, zero missing). The generator's loader for this path remains deliberately fail-soft regardless of the bank's population state — a missing, empty, or partially populated bank directory is read exactly as whatever is currently on disk, with no error and no dependency on a finished run — and the cutout-compositing step still consumes its full, fixed budget of random-generator draws every sample whether or not a bank is present (preserving the stream-alignment discipline below), so training was never blocked on this sweep. The acceptance-audit numbers quoted in §4.7 predate this bank's completion and were measured with dropout-only occlusion; a re-run of that audit against the now-complete bank remains an open item (§7).

**The discriminator is purely geometric in both mechanisms**, and this is the load-bearing rule the whole labelling scheme depends on: a corner's rounded pixel position is tested for membership in any dropout-hole rectangle or against the accumulated cutout alpha mask (threshold $0.5$) — a point-in-region test, computable exactly at generation time, with no legibility heuristic anywhere. This geometric test is deliberately the *only* discriminator, because the labelling policy that depends on it makes a sharp distinction between two degradation modes that must be labelled differently:

- **Photometric degradation** (blur, darkness, noise — the junction is still visible, but its surrounding marker code is not legible) keeps the corner's **true index label anyway**. This is what trains the board-scale context inference (§2, §3.3) at all: identifying a corner from a *different*, legible marker via the lattice, rather than from its own now-illegible one. The hallucination risk this policy accepts is contained entirely at inference time, by the confidence threshold and the lattice-consistency gate (§3.6) — never by softening the training target.
- **Geometric occlusion** (a dropout hole or a cutout object physically covers the junction point, or the point falls outside the frame) removes the corner from **both** targets entirely: not visible, no heatmap Gaussian, no class Gaussian. A covered junction is not a corner, and no amount of context inference should be asked to hallucinate one. In short: occlusion labelling is purely geometric — a junction is unlabelled if and only if the point itself is covered, independent of whether the evidence *around* it remains legible.

### 4.5 Negatives and label schema

With probability $\texttt{negative\_p}=0.05$, a sample carries no board at all — an augmented background only, `board_present=false`, empty corner list — which keeps the class channels honest at zero confidence and the heatmap honest on genuinely empty scenes; this is drawn as the very first random decision in a sample's stream, strictly before any board-dependent sampling, so it can never be perturbed by unrelated generator changes.

The stored label schema is deliberately minimal — analytic labels only, never rendered target tensors, so that target widths ($\sigma_{\text{hm}}$, $\sigma_{\text{cls}}$) stay tunable without regenerating a single sample:
```
{ "image":  <grayscale uint8, input resolution>,
  "board_present": bool,
  "s_px": float,                        # square size at input resolution
  "corners": [ { "x": float, "y": float,   # input-res frame, full float precision
                 "index": int,              # 0..15, §3.2
                 "visible": bool }, ... ] }
```

### 4.6 Determinism

Every random draw in the generation path takes an explicit `numpy.random.Generator` instance — nothing reaches into the global NumPy random state, the standard-library `random` module, or an implicit OpenCV RNG, and this rule is enforced by a static test that scans the source for any bare `np.random.*` call. Fixed-seed validation streams are therefore bit-identical by construction: the detector validation set seeds sample $i$ as `default_rng([val_seed, i])`, with $s_{\text{px}}$ stratified across log-uniform octaves of the training range; the refiner validation set uses `val_seed + 1`, similarly indexed. Training draws an infinite on-the-fly stream seeded per data-loader worker; a resumed run derives its stream seed as $\texttt{train\_seed}\times1000+\texttt{resume\_count}$ (the resume counter stored in and incremented from the checkpoint), so a crashed-and-resumed run is guaranteed not to silently replay its earlier samples (Appendix D). Background images are drawn from a *sorted* file listing specifically because directory globbing order is not itself deterministic across filesystems. Bit-identity is scoped explicitly to a pinned library trio (NumPy, OpenCV, scikit-image versions) plus a content hash of the background corpus file listing, both recorded in every acceptance-audit report for exactly this reason. Two further mechanisms preserve this discipline under feature changes rather than merely at a fixed configuration: the perspective sampler's coin and its three draws (§4.3) and the cutout mechanism's coin and its six per-slot draws (index, scale, rotation, flip, placement, use/skip) both always fire in fixed order regardless of outcome, so enabling, disabling, or reconfiguring either feature never desynchronises any other sampler's draw sequence for a fixed seed.

### 4.7 Generation-quality audit

Before any training run, the generator must clear a fixed battery of gates, run at the standard scale ($n=10{,}000$) against the real COCO background corpus:

| Gate | Measured | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| $s_{\text{px}}$ octave flatness | $0.335 / 0.334 / 0.331$ (three octave shares) | within $\pm20\%$ of an equal $1/3$ share each |
| Negative fraction | $0.0504$ | $0.05 \pm 0.005$ |
| Occlusion incidence | $0.411$ (dropout-hole only, before the cutout bank completed) | within $\pm0.05$ of the configured $0.4$ |
| Round-trip (independently recomposed homography vs. stored corners) | $0.0$ px, over $1{,}000$ samples | $< 0.01$ px |
| Repeatability (fresh subprocess, same seed) | byte-identical, $50/50$ samples | byte-identical |
| Refiner jitter histogram | flat across eight bins of the $\pm3.9375$ px support | within $\pm25\%$ of an equal share per bin |
| Refiner content check (median / p90) | $0.051$ / $0.174$ px | median $\leq 0.10$, p90 $\leq 0.50$ px |

The refiner content check merits its own account, because its first form failed and the failure was root-caused rather than patched over. The check re-labels a sample of refiner crops with classical `cv2.cornerSubPix`, seeded at the crop's known true sub-pixel offset, and compares the classical re-detection to the analytic label; the *original* gate compared against the *maximum* error in the sample and failed at $0.29$ px. Investigation traced this to the classical instrument itself, not the generated labels: `cornerSubPix` is demonstrably ill-conditioned on small-$s$ crops (its $11\times11$ search window is large relative to a marker margin that shrinks with $s$) and on high-anisotropy warp configurations (combined shear and rotation collapsing two quadrants toward a knife-edge saddle point) — a worst-case crop was visually confirmed to be exactly such a degenerate saddle, with the *label* independently exact by the round-trip gate above. The gate was accordingly re-defined on the **median and 90th percentile**, not the maximum, of the classical re-detection error — a systematic geometry or labelling bug (an axis swap, a half-pixel offset) would shift the *entire* distribution, including the median, by at least half a pixel, whereas an instrument-conditioning tail affects only the extreme percentiles of an otherwise-correct sample. Both statistics measured comfortably inside their gates, and this decision — including the explicit acknowledgement that the check's own instrument, not the data, was the source of the original failure — is recorded as methodology (Appendix E) rather than silently absorbed into a looser threshold.

**A configuration note on these numbers.** This audit pass was the last full run of the acceptance battery, executed at the $640\times480$ input configuration in force immediately before this project's native-resolution decision (§3.1, Appendix C); the determinism and round-trip gates are structural properties of the random-generator draw sequence rather than of image content or resolution, and are consequently expected to hold unchanged at the current native $1600\times1200$ configuration, but the full battery has not yet been re-executed and re-reported at that resolution at the time of writing — recorded here rather than silently assumed, and carried forward as an open item in §7, alongside the cutout-bank re-audit noted in §4.4.

## 5. Experiments

### 5.1 Implementation & training details

Two independent training loops (`tools/train_detector.py`, `tools/train_refiner.py`) share primitives — EMA, cosine LR, JSONL logging, checkpointing, no-weight-decay parameter grouping — in `dcc/trainutil.py`, which carries no model-specific imports.

| | Detector | Refiner |
|---|---|---|
| Optimiser | AdamW, $\beta=(0.9,0.999)$ | AdamW, $\beta=(0.9,0.999)$ |
| LR | $3\times10^{-4}$ → cosine → $3\times10^{-6}$ floor | $1\times10^{-3}$ → cosine → $1\times10^{-5}$ floor |
| Warmup | 1000 steps, linear | 200 steps, linear |
| Weight decay | $10^{-4}$, param-grouped (no wd on bias/norm params) | $10^{-4}$, same grouping |
| Batch | $4\times$ accum $8$ = effective 32 | 256 |
| Steps | 250,000 (8 M samples) | 10,000 |
| Precision | bf16 autocast + `channels_last` | bf16 autocast + `channels_last` |
| Grad clip | global-norm 5.0 | global-norm 5.0 |
| EMA decay | 0.999 | 0.999 |

The cosine schedule is a pure function of the step counter, no scheduler object or extra checkpoint state:
$$
\text{lr}(t) = \begin{cases}\text{peak}\cdot t/\text{warmup} & t<\text{warmup}\\ \text{floor} + \tfrac12(\text{peak}-\text{floor})\big(1+\cos(\pi\cdot\tfrac{t-\text{warmup}}{\text{total}-\text{warmup}})\big) & \text{warmup}\le t\le\text{total}\\ \text{floor} & t>\text{total}\end{cases}
$$
Both loops train under `torch.autocast("cuda", dtype=torch.bfloat16)` with no `GradScaler` — bf16 keeps fp32's 8 exponent bits (hence its dynamic range, avoiding the activation/gradient underflow that motivates fp16's loss-scaling machinery) at the cost of fewer mantissa bits, the exact precision trade §3.4's logit-space focal loss is designed to tolerate — and run with a `channels_last` memory format, since the conv-dominated encoder/decoder is the large majority of compute (Appendix A) and channels-last layout lets cuDNN select its faster Tensor-Core convolution kernels directly.

**Measured throughput.** At native resolution ($1600\times1200$, bf16, `channels_last`), the detector's batch ceiling is **4** (batch 8 OOMs cleanly), giving **25.90 samples/s** at peak allocation **20.88 GiB** (reserved 21.73 GiB); gradient accumulation to the effective batch of 32 costs a measured **+0.06%** overhead. At this throughput, the full $8$M-sample schedule is $\approx$**85.8 h** ($\approx3.6$ days) of wall-clock — the training schedule's long pole. A `spawn`-context DataLoader (rather than Linux's default `fork`) is pinned in code, not left as a config default: forking worker processes from a torch-importing parent serialises all child NumPy/OpenCV work through inherited BLAS thread-pool contention, measured at **55 → 404 samples/s** (12 workers, $640\times480$) once switched to `spawn` — a $7\times$ throughput difference from one process-model choice. The full engineering-measurement record (VRAM scaling, resume-determinism verification, checkpoint contract, run-tracking status, and every bug caught during the training-loop implementation) is in Appendix D.

**Validation cadence.** Every 2,500 steps, a fixed 2,000-sample subset yields metrics M-01, M-02, and M-04 (§5.2); every 25,000 steps (and at the final step), the full 10,000-sample validation set runs alongside a checkpoint write. Gate-$\alpha$ and per-block attention-entropy diagnostics (§3.3) are logged at the same cadence via forward hooks that recompute the signal from the model's own public methods rather than re-deriving the gate/attention math independently.

**Freeze-trunk retarget path.** Per the model-level contract, `cls.*` (the class head's own two convolutions) is a disjoint, independently addressable parameter subtree from everything else (`e1..e5`, RoPE, attention blocks, the shared final norm, both gates, the full decoder, and the heatmap head) — a retarget onto a new board's corpus freezes every parameter except `cls.*` (trunk, gates, decoder, and heatmap head transfer unchanged; only the class head retrains, since it is the only board-specific component by construction) and holds frozen BatchNorm layers in `eval()` mode so their running statistics stop drifting on the new corpus. `tools/train_detector.py`'s `--freeze-trunk`/`freeze_trunk` flag now correctly gates the parameter-trainability mask: the `requires_grad_` loop that restricts training to `cls.*` sits inside the same conditional block as the BatchNorm eval-mode switch, so a default (non-retarget) run trains the full network and only an explicit retarget request restricts it to the class head. This is a corrected defect, not a design choice from the start — a short methodology note, since how it was caught is itself informative: an earlier version of this entry point set the trainability mask unconditionally, so *every* invocation, including the default full-network configuration, silently trained only the class head's $149{,}648$ parameters ($\approx2.1\%$ of the network) while the rest of the model sat at its random initialisation for the entire run. A loss that decreases and a run that completes without crashing are both still true when only 2.1% of a network is actually learning, so an ordinary training-loop smoke test could not have caught this; it surfaced during this project's own documentation-and-verification review (Appendix E), a concrete instance of that review discipline catching a defect a functional smoke test alone would miss.

**Precision policy.** All development, training, and evaluation runs at full precision on an RTX 5090 (bf16 mixed-precision, fp32 master weights); no quantisation happens during development. Both networks export cleanly to ONNX at opset 17 via the TorchScript path, with a confirmed op inventory containing zero `Complex`/`Loop`/`If` nodes on either graph (the detector traces to 314 nodes; the refiner, five convolutions and one `DepthToSpace` for its `PixelShuffle`) — every op is TensorRT-standard at this opset, and the RoPE `cos`/`sin` tables trace as concrete ONNX initialisers under the fixed export shape. fp16/INT8 TensorRT engines are produced only at a single, later, gated export step: an engine is accepted only if it passes a parity check against the full-precision reference on 1,000 validation images (corner error $\Delta<0.05$ px, ID accuracy $\Delta<0.1\%$). This split — full precision through all of development, reduced precision only behind an explicit, measured gate — is the same discipline applied to the deployment budget of §6; the complete op-inventory table and the reasoning for why attention/normalisation layers are the first suspects if the parity gate fails are in Appendix D.

### 5.2 Metrics & evaluation protocol

Nine metrics, M-01 through M-09, cover the pipeline end to end, each with an operational definition precise enough to compute unambiguously — several needed sharpening from an original wording that was unmeasurable as stated, recorded here alongside the fix.

**M-01 — Coarse localisation error.** Stage-1 heatmap peaks are matched to ground-truth corners by greedy nearest-neighbour within an $8$ px radius (input resolution); the *tail* statistic is the fraction of *matched* pairs with error exceeding $4$ px, and any ground-truth corner left unmatched within the $8$ px radius counts against M-02 instead. This two-radius definition replaces an earlier, self-contradictory reading that matched at $4$ px and then asked for the tail beyond $4$ px — a quantity that is zero by construction under that matching rule regardless of the network's actual behaviour. The $4$ px threshold is not arbitrary: it is the sub-pixel refiner's hard capture range (§3.5), and the operational rule is a per-deployment $\rho$-budget: at sensor/input ratio $\rho$, the refiner's $\pm4$ sensor-pixel capture range is only $\pm4/\rho$ *input*-pixels of allowance for Stage-1 error, of which integer-peak quantisation alone consumes up to $0.5\rho$ sensor px. At the system's current pinned configuration ($\rho=1$, native resolution), this is the full $\pm4$ px input-side allowance; a tail beyond $1\%$ at whatever $\rho$ is actually deployed blocks the refiner's sign-off, and the prescribed mitigation if it trips is a $3\times3$ quadratic sub-pixel peak estimate before cropping, or a widened refiner support.

**M-02 — Match ratio.** Detected-over-visible-ground-truth-corner ratio, reported by $s$-octave (the same $[16,32)$, $[32,64)$, $[64,128]$ bins as the training-range stratification), since detection behaviour is expected — and, per the operating-envelope analysis (Appendix C), required — to vary strongly across it.

**M-03 — Refined error.** $L2$ error of the Stage-2 soft-argmax readout against the true sub-pixel offset, measured in $1/8$-px units on the dedicated refiner validation stream (never mixed with the detector's own validation set).

**M-04 — ID quality.** Accuracy, plus gated precision/recall at the class-confidence threshold $\tau_{\text{id}}$, over a $16{+}$"none" confusion structure, reported by $s$-octave; the protocol specifically watches for far-channel confusions correlating with strong perspective tilt, since a misread corner two or three lattice steps away from the true one is a materially different failure from a merely low-confidence correct read.

**M-05 — Gate behaviour.** The lattice-consistency gate's demotion rate (identities rejected by the RANSAC fit) and recovery yield (identity-less detections successfully reassigned by lattice projection), tracked per checkpoint — this is the hallucination detector (§3.6) made quantitative, and a rising demotion or recovered fraction over training or across deployment conditions is the system's own signal that the class head is drifting out of its trained distribution.

**M-06 — Pose error.** On the pose-consistent evaluation set (the only place in the whole pipeline camera pose is bookkept during data generation): rotation error in degrees, translation error as a percentage of the board's physical diagonal, with the IPPE two-fold-ambiguity incidence logged as a separate statistic from board-rotational-symmetry confusion — the two are unrelated failure modes and conflating them would hide which one is actually occurring.

**M-07 — Effective-receptive-field probe.** Following Luo et al.'s procedure precisely rather than a simplified single-image version (§2): a unit gradient signal is placed at one class-head output unit and back-propagated to the input, and the resulting $|{\partial \text{output}}/{\partial \text{input}}|$ map is **averaged over at least twenty validation images**, probed at a **central** class-head unit (a border unit's footprint is truncated by the frame edge and is not representative). The protocol reduces the resulting two-dimensional gradient map to a single comparable size via a **radial-mass curve** — cumulative $|{\text{gradient}}|$ mass integrated outward from the query point as a function of radius — read off at the $1\sigma$ and $2\sigma$-equivalent radii Luo et al.'s own variance-addition derivation uses to characterise effective receptive field size, so a trained model's measured footprint and the conv-only ablation's (§5.4, A1) can be compared on the same scalar rather than eyeballed from two heatmaps. A post-attention footprint must span the frame; the conv-only ablation is expected to reproduce a Gaussian of roughly $31$–$62$ px radius — the effective-receptive-field estimate the encoder's own variance-addition arithmetic predicts at initialisation (§3.3) — while acknowledging Luo et al.'s own trained measurement grows the effective footprint by roughly $1.5\times$ over its initial value as training proceeds, so the ablation's acceptance band is judged at convergence, not at an early checkpoint.

**M-08 — Runtime.** End-to-end latency (detector forward pass, batched refiner pass, Stage-3 geometry) at the deployment sensor/input resolution, on the deployment target hardware, reported per stage rather than as a single fused number, so a future regression is attributable to the stage that caused it. Recorded from the first viable checkpoint onward specifically so a runtime regression is visible immediately, not discovered only at final evaluation.

**M-09 — Sim-to-real transfer.** The one metric no synthetic number can substitute for: on a dedicated real-capture evaluation set (never used in training), (a) a well-lit split of at least $200$ frames across the working-distance and tilt range, with corner pseudo-ground-truth from classical OpenCV detection plus sub-pixel refinement, supporting quantitative corner-error and ID-accuracy comparisons; and (b) a degraded split of at least $200$ frames (low light, motion blur, partial occlusion) specifically chosen where classical detection is expected to fail, supporting detection-rate and qualitative gate-behaviour comparison rather than a pseudo-GT numeric one (classical detection has, by construction, no answer to compare against on this split). Before any capture, the printed board must be checked against the locked convention (§3.2) with two items easy to get wrong: `setLegacyPattern=false` and top-left-square-black — a board printed under a pre-4.6 OpenCV default would silently invert the colour convention, corrupting every downstream corner correspondence in a way no per-frame check would catch after the fact.

**Deep-ChArUco-comparable additions.** Two further statistics are computed purely so this system's numbers can be placed directly alongside the one prior publication in this exact problem class, since Deep ChArUco's own datasets were never released and no other published corner-and-ID benchmark on a locked ChArUco board exists: a corner counted correct only if it is within $3$ px of ground truth *and* correctly identified (the paper's exact joint criterion, distinct from this system's own separated M-01/M-04 split), and pose reprojection error $\varepsilon_{\text{re}} < 3.0$ px (the paper's own pose-correctness threshold, Table 3, [Hu2019]). These are additions to, not replacements for, M-01–M-09 — the separated metrics are more diagnostic during development, but a single comparable number is what lets a reader place this system against the only prior result in the field.

### 5.3 Results

No end-to-end trained-model accuracy claim is made anywhere in this paper. Every quantitative number reported so far is one of: a measured component-level quantity (parameter counts, forward latency, per-operation compute, data-pipeline throughput, coordinate-transform correctness on synthetic/analytic test cases), an infrastructure-level smoke test (loss-curve sanity, resume determinism, checkpoint round-tripping), or a stated design argument awaiting the measurement that would confirm it. This reflects where training and evaluation tooling currently stand, honestly:

| Metric | Automated where | Status |
|---|---|---|
| M-01 (coarse localisation error) | in-loop validation, every 2,500/25,000 steps (§5.1) | automated |
| M-02 (match ratio by $s$-octave) | in-loop validation | automated |
| M-03 (refined error, refiner stream) | in-loop refiner validation | automated |
| M-04 (ID accuracy by octave) | in-loop validation (lazy import of the pipeline's own ID-readout function) | automated |
| M-05 (gate demotion/recovery rate) | — | needs a full-pipeline evaluation run over a checkpoint; the sign-off tool does not yet exist |
| M-06 (pose error, pose-consistent set) | — | same; needs the sign-off tool |
| M-07 (effective-receptive-field probe) | introspection tooling (visual/qualitative) | qualitative only so far; the quantitative, twenty-image-averaged form of §5.2 is not yet automated |

No full detector checkpoint currently exists that both (a) was trained with the full network actually trainable (§5.1's freeze-trunk fix removes the obstacle, but no full-network run has yet been executed on the corrected entry point) and (b) has been evaluated by a dedicated sign-off tool covering M-05/M-06. Every number attributed to a "measured" report in this paper is therefore a component-level or infrastructure-level measurement, never an end-to-end trained-model accuracy claim — because no such claim yet exists to report. Closing this gap — a full-network training run, the M-05/M-06 sign-off tool, and the real-capture evaluation set of M-09 — is the immediate next step; §5.4's ablation matrix is the validation plan that runs once it is available.

### 5.4 Ablation study

The validation matrix below is organised by cost tier: eval-only ablations run in hours against the already-trained headline checkpoint; Tier-1 ablations require a full retrain at the cheaper $640\times480$ input (the architectural claims transfer at this size, and it keeps the matrix affordable at $\approx16$ h per run on this project's development GPU); Tier-2 ablations are either native-resolution retrains ($\approx3.6$ days each, matching the headline model's own training cost, §5.1) or a cheap refiner-only retrain. Every ablation is an alternate configuration file plus a run name — no code forks — and every run reports M-01 through M-07 by $s$-octave plus M-06 on the pose-consistent evaluation set, against identical fixed seeds and identical fixed-seed validation sets per input size. Three ablations (marked below) need a small, config-gated option built when they are scheduled, not before. Every result below is stated as a claim and a protocol; none has been run yet, and each entry says so plainly rather than implying otherwise.

A four-run **conference-minimal set** — A1, A6, A5a, A8 — covers the four most novel claims (attention context, the geometric gate, the learned refiner, and perspective training) within one overnight retrain-and-evaluate cycle; everything else extends the written report rather than the presentation.

**Tier 0 — eval-only, no retraining**

**A6 — Lattice gate OFF.** Claim: the lattice-consistency gate converts silent identity hallucination into a detected outlier, and is the sole independent check on learned identities (§3.6). Implementation: an inference-time flag that skips the gate/recovery steps entirely, accepting every confidence-thresholded head identity directly into PnP. Protocol: M-05 (demotion/recovery rates become, without the gate, "admitted hallucinations") and the M-06 pose-error distribution, gated vs. ungated, on the darkest/heaviest-degradation validation bins where hallucination pressure is highest, plus the pose-consistent evaluation set. Expected: near-identical pose on clean bins, heavy-tailed pose corruption on degraded bins once ungated — i.e., the gate should be shown to matter exactly where the system claims to operate, not as a formality. **[result pending]**

**A5a — Learned refiner vs. classical sub-pixel.** Claim: the learned Stage-2 refiner beats classical sub-pixel methods on degraded native-resolution crops, reproducing Deep ChArUco's own headline RefineNet result in this system's regime. Implementation: an inference-time swap of Stage 2 for (i) $3\times3$ quadratic peak interpolation on the Stage-1 heatmap, and (ii) `cv2.cornerSubPix` seeded at the integer peak — no retraining. Protocol: M-03 on the refiner validation stream and end-to-end M-06, broken out by $s$-octave and photometric severity, explicitly reporting both medians and tails given `cornerSubPix`'s own known failure regimes at small $s$ and high-anisotropy warps (§4.7). Expected: parity in bright, large-$s$ conditions; the learned refiner winning tails and degraded bins. **[result pending]**

**A11 — Threshold operating curves.** Claim: $\tau_{\text{hm}}=0.3$ and $\tau_{\text{id}}=0.5$ are sensible, not arbitrary, operating points. Implementation: a sweep, $\tau_{\text{hm}}\in[0.1,0.7]$, $\tau_{\text{id}}\in[0.2,0.8]$, at evaluation time only. Protocol: precision/recall curves for detection (M-02) and gated identity (M-04) by $s$-bin, with the chosen operating points marked; this run also probes the class-map bilinear-readout sub-cell-phase effect (§3.6) directly — if identity recall jumps sharply as $\tau_{\text{id}}$ drops below $0.5$ specifically at cell-corner query phases, that is the documented readout-phase effect, not a threshold miscalibration, and is reported as $p_{\text{id}}$-vs-phase alongside the curves. **[result pending]**

**Tier 1 — retrained at $640\times480$**

**A1 — Conv-only trunk (the headline architectural ablation).** Claim: bottleneck self-attention is the mechanism that makes corner *identity* work beyond the convolutional receptive field — the system's central architectural claim (§2, §3.3). Implementation: `attn_blocks: 0`, everything else identical (data, seeds, schedule). Protocol: M-04 identity accuracy by $s$-octave *at convergence* (the effective receptive field grows during training, so an early-checkpoint comparison would be misleading — judged only at converged checkpoints for both arms); M-05 recovery fraction (the conv-only arm should lean far harder on geometric recovery); and the paired M-07 effective-receptive-field probe (full model: frame-spanning footprint; conv-only: the $\approx31$–$62$ px Gaussian §5.2 predicts). Expected: far-$s$ ($64$–$128$ px) direct-identity collapse in the conv-only arm, near-$s$ parity; localisation (M-01/M-02) essentially unaffected, since it never needed board-scale context. The paired ERF image is the intended conference visual (Appendix F). **[result pending]**

**A2 — Positional encoding.** Claim: relative-offset RoPE is the correct positional scheme, because identity is a relative-geometry inference and translation equivariance matters for partial boards. Two variants: (a) no positional encoding at all (`rope: none`, attention is content-only plus whatever positional leakage survives through convolutional features); (b) ⚙ learned absolute position embeddings (a small option to build when scheduled). Protocol: M-04 by $s$-bin, split by board-centre position in frame (centre vs. edge thirds) and by partial-view fraction. Expected: (a) degraded identity specifically where marker evidence sits far from the query corner (relative-offset binding lost); (b) parity on centred boards but degradation on translated or clipped ones — making the equivariance argument directly measurable rather than asserted. **[result pending]**

**A3 — Skip gates.** Claim: attention-gated skips on the identity path buy precision without the unrecoverable-veto risk the asymmetric-risk argument names (§2, §3.3); the two finer skips must stay ungated. Two variants: (a) `skip_attention_gates: false` (plain concatenation everywhere); (b) `gate_all_skips: true` (gate the finer skips too, pass-through initialised). Protocol: M-02 recall on the darkest brightness bins and smallest $s$-octave (exactly where a learned "looks like board" veto would be most damaging) against false-positive rate; gate-$\alpha$ statistics per checkpoint; final adjudication of variant (b) against real degraded captures once they exist (§7), since synthetic darkness alone cannot clear the sim-to-real veto risk the asymmetric-risk argument is actually about. Expected: (a) mild precision loss, unchanged recall; (b) a precision gain that must prove it costs zero degraded-real recall before being adopted — a burden of proof, not a default assumption. **[result pending]**

**A4 — Ground-truth labelling policy.** ⚙ Claim: labelling photometrically degraded corners with their *true* index (§4.4) is specifically what trains implicit lattice inference. Implementation: a generator extension recording per-sample augmentation-severity flags, and an ablation-only dataloader variant that drops the class target (keeping the heatmap target) for corners in samples beyond a severity threshold — the "dustbin-label the illegible" counter-policy, built only inside this ablation's target rendering so the main pipeline stays heuristic-free. Protocol: M-04 direct-identity accuracy under heavy degradation, M-05 recovery fraction, and attention-map introspection (does the query corner's attention concentrate less on distant markers under the counter-policy?). Expected: the counter-policy model loses direct identity under degradation and leans harder on recovery — quantifying exactly what the true-label policy buys over the more conservative alternative. **[result pending]**

**A7 — Differencing-domain augmentations OFF.** Claim: the glare/ghosting/mixed-brightness extensions (§4.2) matter for the differencing deployment domain specifically (§6), beyond the reference pipeline's literal augmentation list. Implementation: `glare_p: 0, ghost_p: 0`, brightness restricted to multiplicative-only — the reference-pipeline-literal augmentation set. Protocol: synthetic dark/glare bins now; the decisive test is against real degraded captures (§7) once they exist, comparing detection rate, identity accuracy, and gate demotion behaviour. Expected: parity on clean synthetic data; a gap opening specifically on glare- or ghost-afflicted real frames. If no such gap appears once real data exists, the extensions should be retired — the ablation is explicitly designed to cut both ways, not to justify a foregone conclusion. **[result pending]**

**A8 — Affine-only training warps.** Claim: the perspective-warp requirement (§4.3) measurably improves performance on genuinely tilted boards. Implementation: `perspective_p: 0` (the affine-plus-shear sampler alone; the perspective random draws still occur and are simply zeroed, preserving stream alignment with every other configuration, §4.3). Protocol: M-06 pose error on the pose-consistent evaluation set, binned by tilt ($0$–$20°$, $20$–$40°$, $40$–$60°$), and M-01 corner error by tilt (foreshortened-saddle localisation specifically). Expected: monotone divergence with tilt — quantifying, for the written report, exactly what the homography-synthesis change (over an earlier, affine-only design) bought. **[result pending]**

**A10 — Loss formulation.** Claim: the per-channel penalty-reduced focal form is the correct class-head loss (§3.4), because a scalar-weighted or softmax alternative cannot express the "forgive the correct channel, punish every wrong channel at the same cell" asymmetry the design relies on. Two variants: (a) `beta: 0` (plain focal BCE, no penalty reduction — a pure configuration change); (b) ⚙ a $17$-way softmax head (sixteen classes plus an explicit dustbin) with cross-entropy, a small architectural option built when scheduled. Protocol: M-04 accuracy and confusion structure, plus $p_{\text{id}}$-vs-sub-cell-phase curves — the permissive-disc design predicts flat confidence across a correct channel's disc for the focal form, and distance-coded (hence phase-correlated) confidence for the softmax alternative. Expected: (a) noisier class maps near corners; (b) confidence entangled with distance-to-corner, specifically hurting the bilinear readout at off-centre query phases. **[result pending]**

**Tier 2 — native-resolution or otherwise expensive**

**A9 — Input resolution ($640\times480$ vs. $1600\times1200$).** Claim: native-resolution input is what closes the far-range envelope identified in Appendix C (worth its $5.4\times$ training cost and the AGX Orin compute budget it implies, §6). Implementation: the $640\times480$ Tier-1 baseline run doubles as the small-input arm; the native-resolution headline run (already required regardless of this ablation) is the large-input arm — no marginal training cost beyond what the project incurs anyway. Protocol: far-range M-02/M-04 (the $16$–$32$ px octave at native resolution corresponds to sub-envelope scales at $640\times480$); M-06; M-08 measured on both the development GPU and the AGX Orin once available. Expected: the smaller model is blind exactly where the optics chain needs sight, tying the resolution decision directly to the operating-envelope requirement rather than leaving it as an assertion. **[result pending]**

**A5b — Refiner corpus blend (generic corners).** Claim: junction-only refiner crops suffice, and Deep-ChArUco-style arbitrary-corner-shape crops would add generality at the cost of saddle-point specialisation. Implementation: `refiner_generic_frac: 0.3`, a config-gated generic-corner patch generator (default $0$, built when scheduled); refiner-only retrain, $\approx2$ h — cheap because the refiner network is small and its own training run is short (§3.5). Protocol: M-03 on the junction validation stream (does the blend cost specialisation?), optionally against external public sets' non-ChArUco corners (does it buy transfer?). Expected: small or no M-03 cost, some generality gain — informing whether the blend ships in the final model. **[result pending]**

## 6. Deployment application

This application was the original motivation for this system, and it is presented here as exactly that — an application of the general-purpose scale-robust detector above (§3), evaluated on its own compute-feasibility terms, not the paper's framing.

**Context.** The detector's originally intended use is the terminal alignment phase in which an autonomous barrier robot joins an adjacent module — continuous multi-scale tracking and precise spatial control to a secure mechanical connection, outdoors, in variable light and weather. The docking face carries a printed ChArUco board rather than a single ArUco tag specifically because a board over-determines the pose problem: a single tag's four corners give an exactly-determined pose with no way to detect a single corrupted corner (a water droplet, a highlight, ground-in dirt), whereas a board's dozens of corners let a robust estimator — here, RANSAC over the lattice fit (§3.6) — reject corrupted or occluded observations as outliers instead of silently averaging them into the answer. This is the cheapest robustness margin available in the whole application: it costs only the size of the printed target, and it is exactly the redundancy contribution (3)'s lattice gate is built to cash in.

**Sensor and the differencing domain.** The deployment sensor is an OmniVision OV2311 (via the e-con See3CAM\_20CUG module), $1600\times1300$ px at $3.0\,\mu\text{m}$ pitch, monochrome global-shutter, NIR-optimised for a 940 nm illuminator band; its native $4{:}3$ crop ($1600\times1200$) feeds the detector directly at the sensor's own resolution ($\rho=1$, §3.1). To operate outdoors without being blinded by direct sunlight (up to $\sim10^5$ lux, roughly $140\times$ the brightest condition Deep ChArUco was evaluated under, §1), the system actively illuminates the board and alternates illuminator-on and illuminator-off exposures, differencing the pair. The sun's contribution to each frame is the *same* sun $\sim33$ ms apart, so its mean subtracts to exactly zero; what survives is its Poisson fluctuation, added in quadrature between the two frames. The measured radiometric link budget for this optical chain gives a worst-case differenced SNR of $\approx5.7$ per pixel at a $1.2$ m far range ($10$ ms exposure), rising to $\approx54$ per pixel at $0.2$ m — the illuminator need only beat $\sqrt{2\times\text{background}}$, not the background itself, which is why four LED emitters at a conservative datasheet floor close the link inside the IEC 62471 eye-safety exempt limit. The consequence for a corner detector is architectural, not cosmetic: the input the network actually sees is a shot-noise-limited difference image, in which sun-glare-saturated pixels are *dead zones* (zero information after subtraction, not bright regions), and any motion between the illuminator-on/off pair leaves a double-edge ghosting residual concentrated exactly at the square boundaries that carry corner evidence — precisely the domain §4.2's mixed-brightness, soft-glare, and ghosting augmentations exist to cover, since a synthetic corpus that only darkens and blurs a plain frame trains for a domain this camera would never actually produce.

**Compute budget.** The pose rate is set by the differencing architecture itself: a pose per illuminator-on/off frame pair at a 30 fps camera gives 15 Hz pose and a 66 ms/frame-pair compute budget. Against that budget, the detector's measured 833 GFLOPs/frame (§3.3, Appendix A) lands at $42$–$83$ ms on a realistic sustained Jetson AGX Orin TensorRT fp16 throughput of $10$–$20$ TFLOPS — deliberately well below the chip's headline peak figure, the same discipline applied to the RTX 5090 measurements of §3.3, since a real convolutional/attention graph rarely reaches ideal-occupancy dense-matmul throughput — marginal at fp16, comfortably inside budget at INT8 ($21$–$42$ ms, roughly double the sustained throughput). An Orin-NX-class part was assessed and rejected (misses the target at fp16, borderline even at INT8); a Thor-class part would clear it trivially. The refiner's own cost is small by construction and batched across however many peaks Stage 1 returns (capped at top-$K=64$, §3.5): even at the cap, $64\times35$ MMACs $\approx2.24$ GMACs $=4.48$ GFLOPs, under 1% of the detector's own budget. Stage-3 geometry (undistort, RANSAC homography, `solvePnPGeneric`, §3.6) runs on the CPU over at most 16 points and is microseconds-scale on any modern embedded ARM core — none of it is a candidate for the binding constraint, which remains the detector's forward pass throughout.

**Memory.** Combined parameter count is $7{,}124{,}659$ (detector) $+\,97{,}056$ (refiner) $=7{,}221{,}715$: $\approx14.4$ MB of weights at fp16, $\approx7.2$ MB at INT8 (the quantisation level this budget actually targets). No independently measured on-device (or even proxy, single-image-inference) runtime memory figure exists yet for this system — no Jetson-hardware session has been run — so the actual per-engine context memory (typically the dominant term in a small-network TensorRT deployment) is flagged as outstanding measurement work, not estimated here; the closest measured figures are the *training*-time VRAM peaks of §5.1, which are not a usable proxy for a batch-1 inference engine's context size. Precision policy and the TensorRT export parity gate are described in §5.1.

## 7. Limitations & future work

**Refiner training exposure is well short of the lineage's own reference scale.** The sub-pixel refiner's current training recipe exposes it to roughly $5.1$ million crop presentations, against an estimated $\approx95$ million for the reference implementation this system's refiner architecture descends from (§2) — an $18\times$ gap. This is not treated as a defect to silently accept or reflexively fix: the refiner's own validation metric (M-03) is the gate, and the recipe's budget is only escalated if M-03 shows it has not yet converged at the current exposure, rather than matching the reference figure for its own sake.

**The photometric augmentation set omits a step present in the reference pipeline it otherwise follows closely.** The reference augmentation lineage this system's photometric set (§4.2) draws from includes a contrast/saturation/hue jitter step that this project's own set does not currently implement. This gap was caught during the citation-verification pass described in Appendix E, not invented after the fact, and remains open: whether it matters is untested, and it is recorded here rather than silently closed or silently ignored.

**Real-capture evaluation is not yet collected.** Every quantitative number in this paper to date is synthetic or component-level. The dedicated real-capture evaluation set — at least $200$ well-lit frames with classical pseudo-ground-truth, and at least $200$ degraded frames chosen specifically where classical detection is expected to fail (§5.2, M-09) — has not yet been captured. Until it exists, every synthetic metric in this paper certifies internal consistency, not deployment transfer; this is the reason M-09 exists as a distinct, non-optional gate rather than an optional nicety, and several ablations (A3's `gate_all_skips` variant, A7's differencing-augmentation ablation, §5.4) are explicitly *un-adjudicable* until this capture exists, not merely under-evidenced.

**Sim-to-real transfer is consequently untested, not merely unmeasured favourably.** No claim in this paper about real-world detection rate, identity accuracy, or gate behaviour under genuine outdoor lighting should be read as established until M-09 is run; every synthetic result is a necessary but explicitly insufficient condition for deployment readiness.

**Deferred model extensions.** Three natural extensions are identified but not built. A **heteroscedastic uncertainty head** — learning $\sigma_{\text{hm}}$/$\sigma_{\text{cls}}$ per corner rather than training against the fixed $2$ px / $1$-cell widths this system pins as tunable-but-constant design choices (§3.4, §4) — would let the network express its own confidence in a corner's spatial precision rather than relying solely on the separate confidence scalars $p_{\text{hm}}/p_{\text{id}}$. **EPro-PnP-style differentiable pose fine-tuning** (§2) is a deliberate non-adoption for now, not a rejection: once the modular, classically-posed system is stable and validated on real captures, a differentiable-PnP fine-tuning pass over the same intermediate representations is a plausible route to closing residual pose error that the current strictly-modular training (exact intermediate labels, classical PnP only at inference) cannot reach by construction. An explicit **marker-identity head** — predicting which of the twelve ArUco marker codes is legible near a query location, as an auxiliary signal alongside the corner-index class head — is not currently part of the architecture; the current design infers marker-adjacency information only implicitly, through the attention bottleneck's board-scale context and the class head's index predictions, and an explicit auxiliary marker-identity target is a natural, currently unbuilt, extension to make that inference more directly supervised.

**The operating-envelope/optics coupling remains an open three-way trade.** Appendix C's closure analysis resolved the specific collision it found (the far half of the docking approach falling below the detection floor under a previously assumed optics configuration) by moving to native-resolution input, but the general question — board physical size, lens focal length, and network input resolution, jointly, against the full working-distance range — remains an open design coupling rather than a closed one; the minimum working-distance handoff point is recorded as still live and directly coupled to this trade, not resolved by the resolution decision alone.

**The system is locked to one board and one deployment scale.** Retargeting to a different board layout is supported architecturally (the freeze-trunk mechanism of §5.1: the encoder, attention blocks, gated skips, full decoder, and heatmap head all transfer unchanged; only the class head, which is board-specific by construction, retrains), but this path has not yet been exercised end-to-end against a second physical board, and the class-channel count ($16$, from the locked $4\times4$ inner-corner layout) is fixed at training time, not a runtime parameter — a board with a different inner-corner count requires a fresh class head, not merely a fresh retarget run.

**The acceptance audit has not yet been re-run at the current native-resolution configuration, nor against the now-complete object-cutout bank.** As noted in §4.7, the last full battery of generator acceptance gates ran at the $640\times480$ configuration immediately preceding the native-resolution decision, with dropout-only occlusion; while the determinism, round-trip, and stream-alignment properties those gates check are structural rather than resolution- or occlusion-content-dependent, the full battery's re-execution and re-reporting at $1600\times1200$, and against the now-completed object-cutout bank (§4.4), are both outstanding items, not assumed passes.

**No full-network trained checkpoint yet exists.** §5.1 describes a corrected defect in the training entry point that previously restricted every run to training the class head alone; the fix removes the obstacle but a full-network training run has not yet been executed on the corrected code, and the M-05/M-06 sign-off tooling and the M-09 real-capture set (above) are further prerequisites before any accuracy claim can be made. This is the single largest gap between this paper's design arguments and a validated result, and closing it is the immediate next step (§5.3).

## 8. Conclusion

This paper presented ConChArT, a hybrid convolutional–transformer detector, sub-pixel refiner, and classical pose stage for ChArUco board pose estimation, motivated by two structural gaps this paper argues are more fundamental than they first appear: a convolutional-only receptive field that cannot carry corner identity once a board's apparent scale exceeds it, and a training convention — inherited across the SuperPoint/ChArUcoNet/RefineNet lineage — that grants no partial credit near a target. The four contributions address these directly: bottleneck self-attention with axial rotary position embeddings closes the receptive-field gap with a derivation, not just an architecture choice; per-channel penalty-reduced Gaussian supervision replaces one-hot cross-entropy with graded partial reward, licensing an honest "identity unknown" network state; a geometric lattice-consistency gate makes every learned identity independently checkable against classical geometry; and tilt-calibrated homography synthesis with envelope-spanning domain randomisation trains the full scale range the first two contributions are built to exploit. A compact deployment application confirms the resulting network's compute budget is feasible on edge hardware at the target frame rate.

Every architectural and data-engine claim in this paper is backed by a measured component-level number — parameter counts, latency, compute, generation-quality audit statistics, coordinate-transform correctness — verified independently at each stage of this project's own development discipline (Appendix E). What this paper does not yet contain is an end-to-end trained-model accuracy result: no full-network checkpoint has yet completed training on the corrected entry point (§5.1, §7), and the ablation matrix of §5.4 is a validation plan, not a set of results. The honest status of this work is design-motivated and component-verified, with the experiments that would confirm or refute its central claims specified in enough detail to run and reported in enough detail to be judged once they do.

### A.2 HDC no-gridding check

Dilated convolution itself is [Yu2016]'s concept; the hybrid-dilated-convolution (HDC) no-gridding condition of Wang et al. [Wang2018] is $M_i=\max\big[M_{i+1}-2r_i,\ M_{i+1}-2(M_{i+1}-r_i),\ r_i\big]$, $M_n=r_n$, with the design goal $M_2\le K$ ($K=3$ for a $3\times3$ kernel). The four trailing convolutions of e5 have effective dilation rates $(1,1,2,4)$ (the two `double_conv` layers at rate 1, then the explicit $\delta=2,4$ pair). Working backward from $M_4=r_4=4$: $M_3=\max[4-4,\,4-2(4-2),\,2]=2$; $M_2=\max[2-2,\,2-2(2-1),\,1]=1\le3$ — **no gridding**. This also shows the leading rate-1 pair is load-bearing, not incidental: a bare $r=(2,4,\dots)$ group sharing the common factor 2 is exactly the configuration Wang et al. flag as failing ($M_2=4>3$ for $r=(2,4,8)$); the two rate-1 convolutions in front of the dilated pair are what neutralise the common-factor hazard here.

| $W_x$ kernel | repo: $2\times2$, stride 2 (covers every skip position) | $1\times1$, stride 2 | matches the *paper's* stated "1×1" text, not the repo's actual kernel — a divergence between the paper's own equations and its reference code |
| Gating-signal pre-conv | repo's deepest gate: $1\times1$+BN+ReLU conv on $g$ before the gate | none — the gate feeds the raw post-attention/post-LN bottleneck output directly | deliberate: the bottleneck already carries global context; adding a conv here would only cost parameters |
| Output transform $W$ | repo-only $1\times1$+BN applied to $y=x\cdot\alpha$ after the multiply (absent from the paper's own equations) | omitted | protects the exact pass-through init: a BN here would re-centre the pre-sigmoid distribution the bias-driven init depends on |
| $\alpha$ dimensionality | paper's default: scalar per position (its own multi-class variant learns per-channel $\alpha$) | scalar per position | matches the paper's default; per-channel $\alpha$ is a legitimate future ablation, not currently in the matrix |
| Deep supervision | paper trains gates *with* per-scale deep supervision on the segmentation output | none | the H/4 class tap is a partial analogue (a second loss touching an intermediate decoder stage), but not the paper's full per-scale scheme |

Neither the $2\times2$-vs-$1\times1$ $W_x$ kernel choice nor the omitted gating-signal pre-conv changes the pass-through-init guarantee of §3.3 (both are linear/pointwise operations upstream of $\psi$, whose zeroed weight is what actually severs the input dependence) — they are recorded here as site-level departures from the reference implementation, not correctness concerns.

## Appendix B — Derivations

### B.1 The half-pixel resolution map

Under the pixel-centre convention (§3.2), integer index $x$ in a grid denotes the point whose *physical position*, measured in that grid's own pixel-pitch units, is $x+0.5$. Converting that physical position into a second grid whose pitch is $\text{scale}\times$ finer multiplies it by $\text{scale}$; subtracting $0.5$ converts the result back into a pixel-centre-indexed coordinate in the new grid:
$$
x' = (x+0.5)\cdot\text{scale} - 0.5.
$$
Both directions of every resolution change in this system are this one formula with $\text{scale}$ set appropriately — there is no second convention to keep synchronised. Bare scaling, $x'_{\text{naive}}=x\cdot\text{scale}$ (equivalently, the literal "$x/4$" reading of mapping an input-pixel coordinate onto $H/4$ class-map cells), omits both half-pixel terms and introduces a *constant*, resolution-independent bias:
$$
x'_{\text{naive}} - x' = x\cdot\text{scale} - \big[(x+0.5)\cdot\text{scale} - 0.5\big] = 0.5\,(1 - \text{scale}).
$$
At $\text{scale}=1/4$ (the class-map readout) this is $0.5(1-0.25)=0.375$ cells $=1.5$ input pixels — precisely the systematic identity-readout error this system's own critique of the naive formula measured and closed, and precisely why the identity readout (§3.6, B.3) is defined as an `align_corners=False` bilinear grid-sample rather than a literal division.

### B.2 Rotary wavelength spectrum and the no-global-alias proposition

**No-global-alias proposition.** *Claim*: although fast frequency pairs alias routinely by design (a "vernier" effect — one fast pair alone repeats often over the grid), the ensemble 16-dimensional phase code never repeats for two distinct in-grid offsets, provided $\lambda_{\max}\ge2\max(H',W')$ (§3.3). *Proof sketch*: the slowest pair's phase increment over the largest possible in-grid offset is
$$
\omega_{\min}\cdot(\max(H',W')-1) = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{\max}}(\max(H',W')-1) < \frac{2\pi}{2\max(H',W')}\max(H',W') = \pi
$$
— strictly less than a half-turn, over which $(\cos,\sin)$ is injective. Hence the slowest pair's phase is, by itself, injective over every attainable in-grid offset; since the full 16-dimensional code contains this pair as one component, two distinct offsets producing an identical full code would in particular have to agree on the slowest pair too, which the half-turn bound forbids. The fast pairs may each alias individually, but the joint code cannot — the slow pair acts as the vernier's coarse scale, disambiguating which alias-cycle a fast pair's reading belongs to. This is checked both analytically (row-axis phase unaffected by column axis, $\lambda_{\max}\ge2\max(H',W')-\varepsilon$, $\lambda_{\min}\ge2.4$) and empirically: over 2,000 sampled distinct offset pairs, the minimum $L_\infty$ phase-vector distance measured **2.1975 rad**, comfortably over a $10^{-3}$ threshold.

**Precision of the phase tables.** `cos`/`sin` are registered as fp32 buffers, computed once from $(\text{head\_dim}, h, w, \lambda_{\min})$ and cached — never recomputed per forward pass. bf16's 7-bit mantissa is estimated to introduce phase error of up to $\sim0.3$ rad at cell $\approx80$ at the fastest frequency if the phase itself were computed in reduced precision; the reference RoPE-ViT implementation computes phases under `autocast(enabled=False)`, and this system follows the same discipline. The fp32 buffers upcast any incoming bf16 tensor via ordinary type promotion (not autocast's op list) regardless of the surrounding precision context — verified empirically to hold independent of the caller's autocast state.

**On the forfeited extrapolation trade (§3.3).** Because $\lambda_{\max}=2\max(H',W')$ is a function of the *trained* grid, a checkpoint is valid only at its training input size and attend-stage; this is consistent with this system's static-shape ONNX export policy (Appendix D) and costs nothing in practice, since the deployment grid is fixed at export time regardless.

### B.3 The bilinear-readout / grid-normalisation identity

`render_class_targets` (§3.4) maps an input coordinate $x$ to *cell* coordinate $u=(x+0.5)/4-0.5$. PyTorch's `grid_sample` with `align_corners=False`, applied natively in cell-space to a grid of size $W_4$, uses the standard normalised-coordinate formula $g=2(u+0.5)/W_4-1$. Substituting the cell-coordinate definition of $u$:
$$
g = \frac{2(u+0.5)}{W_4}-1 = \frac{2\big(\frac{x+0.5}{4}-0.5+0.5\big)}{W_4}-1 = \frac{2\cdot\frac{x+0.5}{4}}{W_4}-1 = \frac{2(x+0.5)}{4W_4}-1 = \frac{2(x+0.5)}{W}-1
$$
— identical to $g_x$ as stated in §3.6, computed directly from the input-resolution coordinate with no explicit $\div4$ step. Equivalently, with $u$ the cell coordinate: $(2u+1)/(W/4)-1$ expands as $2u+1=2\big(\tfrac{x+0.5}{4}-0.5\big)+1=\tfrac{x+0.5}{2}$, so $(2u+1)/(W/4)-1=\tfrac{(x+0.5)/2}{W/4}-1=\tfrac{2(x+0.5)}{W}-1=(2x+1)/W-1$ — the two sides are algebraically identical, confirming the direct-from-input-coordinate formula and the "go through cell-space first" formula agree exactly. A crafted edge-case test additionally confirms `align_corners=True` **diverges** from the correct convention near the map's edge, independently locking `align_corners=False` as the only convention consistent with the training target.

### B.4 The perspective-calibration derivation

§4.3's projective factor $P$ is constructed so that, to first order in the tilt angle $\tau_{\text{tilt}}$, it reproduces the homography a genuinely tilted fronto-parallel board of apparent scale $s$ would induce in front of a virtual pinhole camera of focal length $f$ — the third-row perturbation $g=\sin(\tau_{\text{tilt}})\cdot s/(f\cdot SQ)$ is exactly this first-order term, conjugated about the render centre so the centre itself is fixed under the map. One documented subtlety in reading this formula: $\psi$ is the axis along which the *foreshortening gradient* runs, not the physical Rodrigues rotation axis of the tilt — those two axes are perpendicular, so the physical tilt axis is $\psi-90°$. This was confirmed by independently constructing a from-first-principles pinhole model (a genuine 3D rotation of a planar board, projected through a pinhole camera) and matching its induced homography against $P$'s own output at the same nominal tilt and azimuth — the agreement is exact at the render centre (machine precision, $\le10^{-9}$ relative) and degrades gracefully with tilt angle exactly as a first-order approximation should, per §4.3's verification table.

### B.5 A worked coordinate trace

To make §3.5–3.6's chain of coordinate maps concrete, trace one corner through Stages 1–2 symbolically at native res ($r{=}1$, so input and sensor coordinates coincide). This illustrates the *arithmetic only* — the intermediate heatmap/refiner/class-map values below are chosen, not measured, purely to exercise every formula with traceable numbers.

1. **Peak.** Suppose the peak-extraction step (§3.5) returns the integer pixel $(802, 604)$.
2. **Crop centre** (§3.5): $r{=}1$, so $c=\mathrm{round}((802+0.5)/1-0.5,\ (604+0.5)/1-0.5)=(802,604)$ exactly — at $r{=}1$ the half-pixel map is the identity on integers, as it must be.
3. **Soft-argmax** (§3.5): suppose the refiner's $64\times64$ output peaks such that the windowed centroid reads $(u^*,v^*)=(35.5,\,27.5)$ — an offset of $+4.0$ output pixels in $u$, $-4.0$ in $v$ from the canonical centre $31.5$. Then $xy_{\text{refined}} = (802,604) + \big((35.5,27.5)-31.5\big)/8 = (802,604)+(0.5,-0.5) = (802.5,\,603.5)$.
4. **ID readout** (§3.6): at native res $W{=}1600$, $W_4{=}400$; $g_x = 2(802.5+0.5)/1600-1 = 1606/1600-1=0.00375$; $g_y=2(603.5+0.5)/1200-1=1208/1200-1\approx0.00667$ — both small positive offsets from the grid centre ($g{=}0$ in normalised $[-1,1]$ coordinates is the map's midpoint, and the pixel-centre geometric middle of a $1600\times1200$ frame is $(799.5,599.5)$; our point $(802.5,603.5)$ sits just past it), exactly as expected for a corner near — but not exactly on — the frame's geometric middle.
5. **Undistort/gate/PnP** (§3.6) then proceed on $(802.5,603.5)$ (pinhole space, identity here since no real distortion is assumed) exactly as those subsections' equations describe, with no further arithmetic specific to this example.

### B.6 Soft-argmax truncation-bias floor, extended

Measured directly against the refiner's own target renderer (§3.5) over $d\in[-3.5,3.5]^2$: worst-case forced-peak readout error **0.1876** grid units (bound 0.35; $\approx0.19/8\approx0.024$ sensor px). A manually constructed narrow Gaussian ($\sigma=0.5$, effectively fully captured by the $5\times5$ window) confirms the orientation convention independently of the width mismatch and recovers to **0.0226** grid units (bound 0.05) — an order of magnitude tighter than the 0.1876 figure, confirming that the larger floor is a property of the window-vs-target-width mismatch ($5\times5$ against $\sigma=1.5$, covering only $\approx1.33\sigma$) rather than a sign or axis-order defect in the readout itself. A second, distinct degeneracy affects the outer $\approx0.4$ px sliver of the $\pm3.9375$ px support: there, the analytic Gaussian target itself is truncated by the finite $64\times64$ canvas edge (independent of the window-vs-$\sigma$ truncation above), pushing worst-case readout error to $\approx0.69$ grid units ($\approx0.086$ sensor px) at the support boundary. Both figures are readout-side floors that exist even with a perfectly trained network and should not be chased as training deficiencies when validation tails are examined near $|d|>3.5$.

## Appendix C — Data-engine parameters and the optics/scale envelope

### C.1 The operating envelope

The training distribution is parameterised by the apparent board-square side $s$ in input pixels (§3.1); every other geometric bound in the system is expressed relative to it, fixed from four physical/statistical constraints:

| Bound | Value | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Marker legibility (sensor-side) | $s \gtrsim 20$–$30$ px | Below it, identity must come from context (the attention path, §3.3) or the Stage-3 recovery pass (§3.6); corners still localise. |
| Class-target separation | $s > 24$ px clean, $s \geq 20$ px tolerable | Soft class-target tails begin to mix below $24$ px. |
| Heatmap peak separation | $s \gtrsim 12$ px | Adjacent corner Gaussians merge below this. |
| Upper scale | none — partial views supported | Placements that clip the frame at large $s$ are sampled deliberately (§4.1). |

These bounds fix the training range $s \in [16, 128]$ px (input resolution, log-uniform) and stratify the fixed validation set across it.

### C.2 Optics closure

Whether a given deployment geometry lands inside this envelope is a direct function of focal length, sensor pitch, board size, range, and the sensor-to-input downsample ratio $\rho$:
$$
s_{\text{input}} = \frac{f}{\text{pitch}}\cdot\frac{S}{z\cdot\rho}
$$
where $f$ is the lens focal length, $\text{pitch}$ the sensor's physical pixel pitch, $S$ the physical board square size, $z$ the working distance, and $\rho = \text{sensor px}/\text{input px}$ the downsample ratio applied before the network sees the frame ($\rho=1$ means the network runs directly on sensor-resolution pixels). This equation, applied against the OV2311 optics settled on for the deployment application (§6, $\text{pitch}=3.0\,\mu\text{m}$), is what drove the resolution decision below. An earlier closure pass against a since-corrected sensor assumption ($6.9\,\mu\text{m}$ pitch, a part later replaced by the OV2311) had already flagged the qualitative problem: the far half of a $1.2$ m docking approach sat below the $s=16$ envelope floor for every board/lens variant then on record — a collision the detector's input resolution alone could not fix, since a lens problem cannot be trained away. Re-run against the corrected OV2311 optics at a then-current $640\times480$/$\rho{=}2.5$ input, the same floor requirement ($s_{\text{input}}\geq16$ at $z=1.2$ m) reduces to $f_{\text{px}}\cdot S\geq48$, closed by either a $60$ mm board with a $\approx12$ mm lens or a $116$ mm board with a $\approx6$ mm lens — but both trade away near-range field of view or far-range framing margin.

**Native-resolution decision.** The cleanest lever removes $\rho$ from the trade entirely: moving the network input from a $640\times480$ downsampled crop to the sensor's own native $1600\times1200$ ($4{:}3$) crop sets $\rho=1$, so the input-side scale floor becomes exactly whatever the optics deliver at the sensor, with no multiplicative downsampling penalty. This is the configuration this system trains and evaluates at (§3.1). It is not free: attending over the resulting feature grid at the encoder's natural $H/8$ stage would mean $30{,}000$ self-attention tokens — more than double the *entire* native-resolution network's total compute if attempted (Appendix A) — so the encoder gains an additional stage and attention runs at $H/16$ instead (§3.3).

## Appendix D — Training-system engineering

### D.1 Loader process model

Forking worker processes from a torch-importing parent (Linux's DataLoader default) serialises all child NumPy/OpenCV work: per-process sample time scales *linearly* with worker count on a controlled benchmark, and setting the thread count to 1 alone does **not** fix it — the mechanism is oneDNN/MKL thread-pool contention across forked children sharing inherited BLAS state, confirmed not to be a CPU-quota artefact since pure-CPU busy loops scale $9.6\times$ across the same worker count on the same machine. Spawning workers instead (`multiprocessing_context="spawn"`) fixes it completely: measured **55 → 404 samples/s** (detector) at 12 workers, $640\times480$. Each spawned worker additionally sets OpenCV's own thread count to 1 (`worker_init_fn`) to prevent OpenCV's internal thread pool from oversubscribing on top of the DataLoader's own worker parallelism. This is pinned in code (`multiprocessing_context="spawn", persistent_workers=True`), not left as a config default a user could silently revert.

### D.2 Native-resolution throughput and memory

Detector training at $1600\times1200$, bf16+`channels_last`, real target shapes: batch ceiling is **4** — batch 8 OOMs cleanly (tried to allocate 5.49 GiB against 4.75 GiB free), and memory scales linearly with batch ($20.88\to$ projected $41.7$ GiB at $B{=}8$, not a fragmentation artefact) — **25.90 samples/s** at $B{=}4$, peak allocation **20.88 GiB** / reserved 21.73 GiB. Gradient-accumulation to effective batch 32 (accum $=8$) costs **+0.06%** overhead (25.90 vs. 25.92 samples/s, per-microbatch vs. true macro-step) — AdamW's own cost is noise beside the forward/backward cost at this resolution. $8{,}000{,}000$ samples $/$ 25.9 samples/s $\approx85.8$ h $\approx3.57$ days — the schedule's long pole, $5.42\times$ the wall-clock of the $640\times480$/H-8 variant's 15.8 h for the same sample count. Generation still comfortably out-feeds the GPU: projected 82.76 samples/s at 12 workers (scaled from the measured $640\times480$ spawn-fix factor of $8.726\times$) against the GPU's 25.9 samples/s — the GPU remains the bottleneck at both resolutions. At batch 4, no other large resident process shared the GPU during this benchmarking session (0 MiB held by other processes), leaving $\approx8.4$ GiB of slack against a future competing process before needing to drop to $B{=}2$ (measured at the *same* 25.6 samples/s, since GPU utilisation, not batch size per se, is the binding constraint).

### D.3 Compile flag and resume determinism

**`torch.compile`** was measured, not assumed off by default on aesthetic grounds: at $640\times480$/H-8 (conv-only), `torch.compile` gained **+7%** throughput (177 vs. 166 samples/s, $B{=}32$); once attention blocks were added, the same knob measured **$-1.5\%$** (139.6 vs. 141.7 samples/s, $B{=}32$) — the compile-time and fragility cost is no longer paid back by a hybrid conv-transformer graph at this scale, so the config default is `off`, with the measurement rather than a general "compile is risky" heuristic as the reason.

**Resume semantics.** Stream seed $=\text{train\_seed}\times1000+\text{resume\_count}$, with `resume_count` stored in the checkpoint and incremented on every resume. This prevents a specific replay bug: the training stream seeds `default_rng([seed, worker_id])`, so a naive resume (same seed) would restart the *identical* infinite sample sequence from its beginning, silently re-training on the earliest samples a second time while never reaching fresh data beyond whatever the longest uninterrupted run previously covered. Verified bit-identical: a fresh 30-step run followed by a resume-and-extend to 35 steps printed `stream_seed=2000001 resume_count=1 start_step=30` (exactly `train_seed(2000)×1000+1`), and steps 31–35 reproduced **identical** losses (238.938/256.941/238.605/247.778/264.339) across two independent process invocations (the second after an unrelated mid-validation OOM and retry).

**Learning-rate provenance.** Two independent design passes proposed different detector peak learning rates before the architecture settled: an initial pass recommended AdamW $5\times10^{-4}$ (CornerNet/CenterNet lineage precedent, $2.5$–$5\times10^{-4}$); a later, architecture-specific pass both re-derived $3\times10^{-4}$ from first principles for a hybrid conv-transformer graph (pre-norm transformer blocks are typically more LR-sensitive than a pure conv stack, favouring the smaller of the two candidate values) and confirmed $3\times10^{-4}$ as the value ultimately adopted — the better-justified choice for this architecture, not a compromise between the two proposals. Warmup (1000 steps, linear), EMA decay 0.999, and grad-clip 5.0 were carried unchanged from the original training recipe throughout this process.

### D.4 Checkpoint contract and run tracking

One `.pt` file per checkpoint event, with **9** keys: `step`, `resume_count`, `model` (full `state_dict`), `ema` (shadow `state_dict`), `optim`, `cfg` (frozen config snapshot), `git_hash`, `torch_rng` (`torch.get_rng_state()`), `last_val`. **Deliberate NumPy-global exclusion.** No NumPy global RNG state is captured, by design: every NumPy draw in this project's generator code flows through an explicit `np.random.Generator` seeded from config, so NumPy's legacy global RNG carries no reproducibility-relevant state at all — capturing it would be dead weight, and the codebase's zero-global-RNG purity rule (statically enforced by a source scan, §4.6) would flag any code path that *did* rely on it. An earlier version of this checkpoint format did include a `numpy_rng` key, independently flagged during this project's own review discipline (Appendix E) as a real violation of exactly this purity rule — the key has since been removed from the checkpoint save/load functions, resolving the flagged violation by eliminating the offending mechanism rather than widening the test's allowlist. `EMA`'s own state round-trips the *full* model state (parameters and buffers, including non-floating-point buffers like BatchNorm's tracked-batch counters, which are copied undecayed rather than averaged) — verified bit-for-bit on both a resumed run's model weights and the restored global torch RNG (7 assertions passing, including a checkpoint round-trip and a `restore_optim=False` path for the freeze-trunk retarget scenario of §5.1).

**Run tracking, landed vs. planned.** Each run writes to `runs/<name>/`: `metrics.jsonl` (one JSON object per logged event — per-step `{step, loss, lr, grad_norm, samples_per_s}` and per-validation records) and `ckpt_<step>.pt` files, confirmed directly against the actual smoke-run directory shipped with this repository. No experiment-tracking service is used, by design — greppable, diffable JSON lines and ordinary checkpoint files. Two pieces of the originally planned tracking setup are not, at the time of writing, present in the landed code: a separate frozen `config.yaml` copy per run directory (the config snapshot exists only *inside* each checkpoint's `cfg` key), and a `metrics.jsonl`-to-plot utility — neither exists in the tooling directory at the time of writing, consistent with the evaluation-tooling gap noted in §5.3.

### D.5 Bugs found and fixed during the training-loop implementation

Four issues were caught before landing, none surfaced by a bare "does it crash" smoke test alone:

1. **Scalar/tensor mismatch at the loss call site.** The detector loss's visible-corner-count argument is used as a Python scalar internally, but the trainer's first draft passed the raw per-sample `(B,)` visible-count tensor directly — calling `float()` on a multi-element tensor raises. Fixed by summing before the call at both training and validation call sites.
2. **Missing channel-axis alignment for the heatmap target.** The heatmap target is stored as `(B,H,W)` — no explicit channel axis — while the detector's heatmap logits are `(B,1,H,W)`. The refiner loss already unsqueezes its own single-channel target defensively (§3.5); the detector loss does not perform the analogous unsqueeze internally. Passing the raw `(B,H,W)` target would silently left-pad-broadcast against the logits' *channel* dimension rather than its batch dimension, producing a `(B,B,H,W)` cross-product that pairs every sample's prediction against every *other* sample's target — the identical failure shape as the refiner-loss bug of §3.4/3.5, at the opposite call site. Fixed by unsqueezing at the trainer's own call site; flagged for the loss module's own owner to consider adding the same internal unsqueeze the refiner loss already has, so future callers cannot reintroduce the same class of bug.
3. **Attention-entropy-probe OOM.** A batched sweep of the attention-entropy diagnostic at $T\approx7500$ was found, during smoke testing, to attempt a $\sim14$ GB allocation and OOM the entire run. Fixed by restricting the entropy hook to a single image per validation pass (the gate-$\alpha$ hook is cheap enough to run over a whole batch, and remains batched) and wrapping the entropy probe in an out-of-memory exception handler as a second line of defence, since a crashed diagnostic must never take down a real multi-hour run.
4. **Throughput metric diluted by validation wall-time.** `samples_per_s` was computed as total samples over total elapsed time throughout, but the elapsed-time clock started once before the training loop while validation (called inline in the same loop) can take far longer than a single training step; after the *first* validation, every subsequent step's logged throughput was diluted by all past validation wall-time even though the sample count itself only counts training samples. Fixed in both training entry points by tracking validation time separately and excluding it from the elapsed-time denominator throughout, with the training/validation wall-clock split additionally reported at run end.

The freeze-trunk trainability-mask defect described in §5.1 is a fifth, later-caught issue in the same entry point, caught by this project's documentation-and-verification review rather than the training-loop implementation pass itself (Appendix E).

### D.6 ONNX / TensorRT export detail

Both networks export cleanly at opset 17 via the TorchScript path; the newer dynamo-based export path was found broken for this graph under the torch version used in this project and was not pursued further once the TorchScript path was confirmed to succeed cleanly. Confirmed op inventory (`onnx.checker.check_model` passes on both graphs; a dedicated test additionally asserts zero `Complex`/`Loop`/`If` nodes):

| Detector (314 nodes) | Refiner |
|---|---|
| `Conv`(26), `MatMul`(12), `LayerNormalization`(5), `Softmax`(2), `Sigmoid`(2), `MaxPool`(4), `Resize`(6), `Erf`(2), `Add`(24), `Mul`(34), `Relu`(20), `Concat`(11), `Reshape`(6), `Slice`(13), `Transpose`(8), `Gather`(10), `Cast`(4), `Sqrt`(6), `Sub`(4), `Div`(8), `Constant`(76), `Identity`(22), `Shape`(9) | `Conv`(5), `DepthToSpace`(1), `Relu`(4), `Slice`(2), `Constant`(8), `Identity`(2) |

Every op is TensorRT-standard at this opset; `LayerNormalization` and native `Softmax`/scaled-dot-product decomposition are opset-17 benefits taken directly, and TensorRT re-fuses the decomposed attention pattern into a native multi-head-attention kernel; `PixelShuffle` traces to `DepthToSpace`, also TensorRT-native. **Static shapes**: export uses a fixed input size, matching the fixed attend-stage of §3.3 — the RoPE `cos`/`sin` buffers are precomputed at construction time for one specific $(H',W')$ and traced as ONNX initialisers (concrete constant tensors in the exported graph, even though non-persistent in the PyTorch model state); no in-graph table-computation is attempted under dynamic shapes, which would be fragile for TensorRT with zero benefit here since the deployment grid is fixed at export time anyway. The `grid_sample` call and all Stage-3 logic (§3.6) stay entirely in pipeline code, outside both exported network graphs.

**Why attention/normalisation layers are the first suspects if the export parity gate (§5.1) fails.** SDPA's softmax and both LayerNorms are the first genuinely precision-sensitive operations this graph introduces beyond the conv stack's already fp16-safe BN/ReLU/sigmoid — a softmax over $T=7500$ logits accumulates a sum of exponentials whose relative rounding error compounds across all $T$ terms, and LayerNorm's variance computation subtracts a mean from every element, a classic catastrophic-cancellation site at reduced precision if the feature scale is large. Both are already computed internally at fp32 accumulation by their respective CUDA kernels regardless of the surrounding autocast precision — the parity gate exists to confirm that this internal-accumulation safety net, plus the fp32 RoPE phase tables of §3.3/B.2, together suffice once the *weights themselves* are quantised at export. If the parity check fails, the fallback is TensorRT's per-layer precision API on the offending layer, not a wholesale retreat to fp32 for the whole engine — the point of gating at individual-layer granularity is to keep as much of the network as possible at the faster precision while isolating the correction to wherever it is actually needed.

### D.7 Determinism policy

Data streams are made bit-identically reproducible by construction (explicit seeded generators throughout, §4.6); CUDA kernel-level determinism is deliberately **not** enabled, since several operations in this graph (bilinear upsample's backward pass among them) have no deterministic CUDA kernel, and the atomics-level nondeterminism this leaves is accepted — the part of reproducibility that actually matters for auditing a run (which samples were seen, in which order, resumable without replay) is guaranteed at the data-stream level, independent of kernel-level bitwise reproducibility.

## Appendix E — Reproducibility and verification protocol

This system was built under a discipline that treats verification as load-bearing engineering, not a final check: every convention was locked and tested before the code that depends on it was written, every architectural choice was measured in a throwaway prototype before it was committed to production code, every implementation unit was independently re-gated by a second pass before being accepted, and every citation this design rests on was re-read from the primary source rather than carried forward from memory. Each of these is described below with the concrete evidence it produced, not as an abstract claim.

**Convention-lock, test-first.** The board's analytic geometry, corner-index convention, and target-rendering functions were locked and covered by regression tests (§3.2's `cornerSubPix`-agreement and marker-identity checks among them) *before* the compositing engine that depends on them was written, precisely because every other coordinate convention in the system — the half-pixel resolution map (§3.2, B.1), the refiner's crop-centring, the class-map readout — is downstream of getting this one contract right first. Every subsequent development stage followed the same order: pin the contract, write the tests the contract implies, then implement against them.

**Measured prototype before production.** Both major architectural pivots in this project — the addition of the self-attention bottleneck, and the move to native-resolution input — were first built as small, throwaway benchmark scripts and measured directly on the development GPU (parameter counts, forward/backward latency, VRAM headroom against other resident processes, numerical stability under mixed precision) *before* a single line of the corresponding production module was written. The production network's measured parameter count ($7{,}124{,}659$, §3.3/Appendix A) matches its own prototype's target to $0.001\%$ precision specifically because the production implementation was built to reproduce a benchmark that had already been run, not the other way around.

**Independent re-verification of every implementation unit.** Every implementation unit was accepted only after a second, independent pass re-ran its test suite from a clean process, read the diff line by line at every seam a convention could silently drift at, and — where a numeric acceptance gate existed — reproduced the measurement independently rather than trusting the implementing pass's own report. This discipline caught concrete defects before they reached the corpus: a systematic NumPy 2.x float64-promotion bug in the photometric pipeline (roughly $91\%$ of samples affected, caught before the first training run and fixed with an $11\%$ generation-speed improvement as a side effect); a random-generator seed collision between two independently authored validation-stream constructors; and, at the Stage-3 inference pipeline, a recovery-pass bug in which two different identity-less detections could both claim the same recovered corner index (§3.6), reproduced with a two-point synthetic counter-example before being fixed and locked behind a permanent regression test. The freeze-trunk trainability-mask defect of §5.1/D.5 is a later instance of this same discipline catching a real defect a functional smoke test alone would not have surfaced.

**Primary-source citation verification.** Beyond ordinary code review, every load-bearing design citation named in §2/§3 — every paper and every reference implementation — was independently re-fetched and re-read in full (primary-source text and, where one exists, the actual reference code, not a summary of either), with every claim required to carry a verbatim quote or code line, not a paraphrase from memory. This caught defects a citation-trusting review would not have: the widely repeated "$\approx12$ million training composites" exposure figure (§2) does not appear anywhere in the Deep ChArUco paper and was traced to a different source's checkpoint naming; the reference augmentation pipeline this project otherwise follows closely turned out to include a contrast/saturation/hue jitter step this project's own photometric set (§4.2) had silently omitted — recorded here and in §7 as a still-open item, not yet closed; and OpenCV's IPPE pose solver was confirmed, by reading its actual C++ source rather than its documentation, to silently swallow internal solver failures and return zero solutions — a defined, reachable event the Stage-3 pipeline now explicitly handles and regression-tests (§3.1, §3.6), rather than an assumption that would otherwise have produced an unhandled exception the first time a genuinely degenerate correspondence set reached the solver in the field.

**Pre-flight verification before any full training commitment.** Before any full-scale training run is launched, a fixed battery of lightweight checks is run against a fresh model instance, each targeting one specific failure mode that would otherwise only surface after hours of wasted compute:

- *Predicted-vs-observed initial loss.* The heatmap and class-head logit biases are initialised to $-2.19$ (§3.3) specifically so the initial loss is analytically predictable — on the order of the number of background pixels times a small penalty term, not the $10^3$–$10^4\times$ inflation a naive zero-bias initialisation would produce. This was checked directly: a $300$-step sanity run on synthetic noise images measured an initial loss of $48.37$, closely matching the value the bias initialisation predicts, and a monotone decrease to $0.022$ over those $300$ steps with no NaN or Inf anywhere in the loss or any parameter gradient.
- *RoPE relativity and no-global-alias.* The rotary position encoding's core guarantee — that its attention product depends only on content and relative offset, and that no two distinct in-frame token positions ever produce an identical joint phase code (§3.3, B.2) — is checked analytically (the slowest wavelength's phase increment over the full token grid extent is confirmed to stay below one full cycle) and empirically (a sampled check over thousands of distinct token-position pairs confirms no near-collision in the full multi-frequency phase vector).
- *Gradient balance.* The class-head loss weight $\lambda_{\text{cls}}=1.0$ is calibrated, not assumed: the heatmap and class targets have exactly equal pixel/cell counts by construction ($1{,}920{,}000$ elements each side, at native resolution's full-frame heatmap vs. the $H/4$, sixteen-channel class map, §3.4), so an initial-loss balance check confirms neither head's gradient dominates the shared trunk by sheer element count before training begins; a companion check confirms every parameter along both the attention path and the gated-skip path receives a nonzero gradient from a single backward pass, ruling out an accidentally disconnected path before it wastes a training run. Attention specifically was confirmed *not* collapsed to a uniform, content-blind distribution over the same $300$-step sanity run: per-block attention entropy measurably differentiated from its near-uniform initial value.
- *Refiner zero-offset closure.* The refiner's readout convention ($u^\ast = 31.5 + 8d$, soft-argmax back to $d$, §3.5) is checked for exact algebraic closure at zero offset and across the sampled jitter range before training, so a systematic half-pixel or axis-order bug in the crop-centring or readout chain cannot silently masquerade as ordinary training noise.
- *One-batch overfit capability.* A network that cannot drive its loss toward zero on a single, fixed, tiny batch has a wiring defect in its loss, optimiser, or backward path that no amount of full-scale training will fix; this check is part of this project's standard pre-launch discipline and is run before every full training commitment, distinct from (and cheaper than) the $300$-step multi-sample sanity run described above.
- *Translation equivariance.* The design argument for RoPE over an alternative positional scheme (§2, §3.3) rests on the query-key dot product depending only on relative, not absolute, token offset; this property is checked directly on the attention module in isolation (a shifted-input probe reproduces the same relative-offset attention pattern) as a targeted unit check, separate from the whole-network sanity runs above.

Where this section describes a check that is part of this project's standing pre-launch discipline rather than one this paper can quote a specific already-executed number for, that is stated plainly rather than left ambiguous — the discipline is applied at every full-scale launch, and its role here is methodological description, not a results table.
